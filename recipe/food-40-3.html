Butter Tarts

Canada

Canada's most argued-over pastry: flaky tart shells filled with a molten mixture of butter, brown sugar, egg, maple syrup and vanilla, with the great divide — raisins or pecans or plain, runny or firm — debated in every bakery lineup. The filling should wobble when it leaves the oven and set to gooey, never rubbery. Blind-bake the shells well: a soggy base is the only universally agreed failure.

Ingredients

  • 12 tart shells (flaky pastry, blind-baked golden)
  • Filling: 100 g butter + 200 g brown sugar + 2 eggs + maple syrup + vanilla + pinch of salt
  • Raisins or pecans (or neither) + splash of vinegar (optional, anti-crystallization)

Method

  1. Roll pastry thin; line tart tins; chill; blind-bake until pale gold.
  2. Melt butter with brown sugar; cool slightly; whisk in eggs, maple and vanilla.
  3. Scatter raisins/pecans in shells; fill two-thirds (they bubble up).
  4. Bake at 200 °C for 12–15 minutes until edges set but centers wobble.
  5. Cool in tins — they firm as they cool; serve barely warm or room temp.

Chef tips

  • Centers must wobble — fully set tarts are overbaked.
  • Fill two-thirds only — the filling puffs then settles.
  • Blind-bake properly — raw pastry plus liquid filling equals mush.
  • Runny vs firm is personal — 12 minutes runny, 15 firm.
