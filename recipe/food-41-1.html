Brik

Tunisia

Brik à l'œuf is Tunisia's shattering street-food triangle: whisper-thin malsouka pastry wrapped around a whole raw egg with tuna, capers, parsley and potato (or minced meat), flash-fried until blistered gold — the egg just set at the edges, gloriously runny in the middle. Eaten with lemon and a specific technique (bite the corner, let the yolk run, catch it), it is Ramadan's essential starter and the beach snack of Tunisian summers. Fry to order: a held brik is a sad brik.

Ingredients

  • 8 malsouka/spring-roll sheets
  • 8 eggs (1 per brik) + 2 cans tuna + capers + parsley + boiled potato
  • Oil at 175–180 °C + lemon + harissa to serve

Method

  1. Lay out one sheet; place tuna, capers, parsley and a little potato off-center.
  2. Crack a whole egg over the filling; fold quickly into a triangle and seal.
  3. Slide immediately into 175–180 °C oil — work one at a time.
  4. Fry 2–3 minutes, turning once, until blistered and gold.
  5. Drain; serve instantly with lemon and harissa.

Chef tips

  • One at a time — assembled briks leak if they sit.
  • Seal well — escaping egg makes oil volcanoes.
  • Runny center is correct — fully set yolk is overcooked.
  • Eat immediately, over the plate — yolk will run.
