Tarator

Bulgaria

Tarator is Bulgaria's ice-cold summer soup — thick yogurt thinned with cold water, loaded with diced cucumber, crushed garlic, fresh dill, a thread of olive or sunflower oil, and a crunch of chopped walnuts. No cooking required: everything is simply whisked together and chilled, and in Bulgaria it is served in bowls or even pint glasses as a drinkable cooler. Bulgaria's native yogurt cultures (Lactobacillus bulgaricus) give it a distinctive tang that makes this the most refreshing fifteen minutes in Balkan cooking.

Ingredients

  • 500 g thick full-fat plain yogurt + 400–500 ml ice-cold water
  • 2 cucumbers, peeled and finely diced + 2 garlic cloves, minced
  • 30 g fresh dill + 75 g walnuts, chopped + 3 tbsp oil + salt + pepper

Method

  1. Whisk the yogurt smooth, then add cold water gradually until soup-thin but still creamy.
  2. Fold in cucumber, garlic, most of the dill and walnuts, plus the oil.
  3. Season boldly with salt and pepper — cold soup needs more salt than you think.
  4. Chill at least 30 minutes (an hour is better) for flavors to marry.
  5. Serve ice-cold, garnished with remaining dill and walnuts.

Chef tips

  • Add water gradually — you can always thin more, but can't thicken back.
  • Fresh dill only — dried dill tastes dusty against the cool yogurt.
  • Add walnuts just before serving so they stay crunchy.
  • Use thick yogurt — thin yogurt makes watery tarator.
