Makroudh

Tunisia

Kairouan's famous date pastries: semolina dough wrapped around spiced date paste, cut into diamonds, fried (or baked) and soaked in honey-orange-blossom syrup. Tunisia's makroudh is diamond-cut and honey-soaked, distinct from Morocco's baked cornes; the semolina gives a sandy, rich crumb that drinks syrup beautifully. They keep for weeks, improving as the honey settles — the classic Eid and wedding sweet.

Ingredients

  • Dough: 500 g fine semolina + 150 ml oil/butter + orange-blossom water + pinch of salt
  • Filling: 400 g date paste + cinnamon + cloves + orange zest + butter
  • Honey syrup: honey + orange-blossom water + lemon

Method

  1. Rub semolina with oil/butter until sandy; rest 30+ minutes.
  2. Bind with blossom water to a pliable dough; rest again.
  3. Roll date paste with spices into ropes; wrap in dough logs.
  4. Flatten slightly; cut diamonds; fry at 170 °C until gold (or bake).
  5. Soak hot pastries in warm honey syrup; drain on a rack.

Chef tips

  • Rest the semolina — unhydrated grains fry gritty.
  • Seal the date ropes fully — exposed paste leaks into the oil.
  • Hot pastry + warm honey — cold soaking barely penetrates.
  • They improve for days — make ahead of any feast.
