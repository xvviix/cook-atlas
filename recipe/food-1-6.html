Osso Buco

Italy

Osso Buco alla Milanese: cross-cut veal shanks (with the marrow-filled bone, the 'buco') dusted in flour, seared, then braised low and slow with soffritto, wine, stock and tomato until the meat falls from the bone. Finished with gremolata (parsley, garlic, lemon zest) and classically served over saffron risotto.

Ingredients

  • 4 veal shanks, bone-in, 3–4 cm thick (~300 g each)
  • Flour for dredging
  • Olive oil + a knob of butter
  • 1 onion, 1 carrot, 1 celery stalk — all finely chopped; 2 garlic cloves
  • 2–3 tbsp tomato paste (or 400 g chopped tomatoes)
  • 125 ml dry white wine
  • 500 ml veal or chicken stock
  • 1 bay leaf, thyme/rosemary
  • Gremolata: parsley + garlic + grated lemon zest, finely chopped

Method

  1. Score the membrane around each shank in 2–3 places so it doesn't curl; season and dredge lightly in flour, shaking off excess.
  2. Sear the shanks in hot oil/butter until deeply browned on both sides; set aside.
  3. Soften the soffritto in the same pan, stir in tomato paste 1–2 minutes, then deglaze with wine and add stock, herbs and tomatoes.
  4. Return the meat, cover, and braise gently — oven at 160–175 °C or stovetop at a bare simmer — 1.5–2 hours, turning once, until spoon-tender.
  5. Mix the gremolata just before serving; uncover for the last 15 minutes if a thicker sauce is wanted.
  6. Plate shanks with sauce over Risotto alla Milanese, shower with gremolata, and serve the marrow with a small spoon.

Chef tips

  • The flour dredge both browns the meat and thickens the sauce — don't skip it.
  • Low and slow is essential; boiling makes shanks tough instead of silky.
  • Gremolata must be fresh and added at the end — it lifts the whole dish.
