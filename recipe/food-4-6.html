Bifana

Portugal

Portugal's pork-steak sandwich: thin cutlets marinated in white wine, garlic, paprika and bay, fried in lard/butter and simmered in the marinade until the juices become the sauce, then stuffed into a crusty papo seco roll — often with mustard or piri-piri. The rival of the francesinha in every tasca debate.

Ingredients

  • 500 g pork loin, sliced very thin (pounded)
  • Marinade: 120 ml white wine, 4 garlic cloves (crushed), 1 bay leaf
  • 1 tbsp massa de pimentão (red pepper paste) or sweet paprika + chili
  • Thyme, salt, pepper
  • Lard + butter for frying
  • 4 papo seco/crusty rolls; mustard + piri-piri to serve

Method

  1. Marinate the pounded cutlets 2+ hours, ideally overnight, refrigerated.
  2. Sear the cutlets fast in foaming lard-butter, ~2 minutes per side; set aside.
  3. Pour in the marinade with the garlic; reduce 8–10 minutes to a glossy, spoonable sauce.
  4. Return the pork to glaze in the sauce 1–2 minutes.
  5. Pile pork + sauce into warm rolls; serve with mustard and piri-piri on the side.

Chef tips

  • Pound thin = tender and sauce-hungry; thick bifanas are a common failure.
  • The sauce should soak the bread — a dry bifana is a sad bifana.
  • Vendas Novas (Alentejo) style with white wine is the benchmark; Porto versions run spicier.
