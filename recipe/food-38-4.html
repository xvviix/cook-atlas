Mahshi

Egypt

Egyptian mahshi is a whole family of stuffed vegetables — peppers, zucchini, eggplant, cabbage and vine leaves — filled with a vibrant vegetarian hash of short-grain rice, tomato, onion and a mountain of parsley, dill and cilantro, then braised in tomato-scented broth until glistening. Friday's labor of love, made assembly-line with family, it is served warm with yogurt or cool as leftovers. The herb quantity surprises newcomers: mahshi filling should be green-flecked through and through.

Ingredients

  • Mixed vegetables for stuffing + 2 cups short-grain rice
  • Filling: 1 onion + tomato sauce + 1 cup parsley + ½ cup cilantro + ⅓ cup dill + mint + cumin
  • Braising: hot stock + tomato + butter/oil + lemon

Method

  1. Hollow zucchini, peppers and eggplant; blanch cabbage and vine leaves.
  2. Mix raw rice with sautéed onion-tomato and all the fresh herbs.
  3. Fill loosely — three-quarters full, since rice swells.
  4. Pack snugly in a pot; pour over hot tomato stock to nearly cover.
  5. Weight with a plate; simmer 40–50 minutes until rice is tender.
  6. Rest 15 minutes; serve warm with lemon and yogurt.

Chef tips

  • Fill three-quarters only — overfilled mahshi burst.
  • Fresh herbs in quantity — dried herbs can't substitute.
  • Short-grain rice — it cooks creamy inside the vegetables.
  • Pack tightly — loose mahshi tumble and unwrap.
