Šakotis

Lithuania

Šakotis ('branched tree') is Lithuania's spectacular spit-cake: drippy egg-butter batter ladled layer upon layer onto a rotating spit over open fire (or oven), each layer setting into golden spikes and branches like a fir tree. A wedding and Christmas centerpiece that keeps for months, it's made from little more than eggs, butter, sugar, flour and cream — the magic is all in the rotation. Home oven versions use a draped-batter method; purists rent the spit.

Ingredients

  • 10 eggs + 250 g butter + 250 g sugar + 250 g flour + cream + vanilla + lemon zest

Method

  1. Cream butter with sugar until pale; add eggs one by one.
  2. Fold in flour, cream, vanilla and zest to a thick pourable batter.
  3. Heat the spit/oven setup; start the rotation.
  4. Ladle thin layers, letting each set and drip before the next.
  5. Build 30–45 minutes until golden and branched.
  6. Cool on the spit; slide off; store dry — it keeps for months.

Chef tips

  • Thin layers — thick ladles slide off instead of spiking.
  • Constant rotation — still spots burn and sag.
  • Patience — the tree builds slowly.
  • Store dry — humidity softens the spikes.
