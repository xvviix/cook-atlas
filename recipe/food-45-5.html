Manakish

Syria

Manakish (manousheh) is the Levant's breakfast flatbread: soft yeasted dough dimpled with fingertips, spread with za'atar mixed to a paste with olive oil (or with akkawi cheese, or lahm bi ajeen meat), and baked blazing-hot until puffed and spotted. Eaten warm, folded around cucumber, tomato, mint and olives with tea, it's Syria's morning ritual. A screaming-hot oven or pizza steel gives the puff-and-char of a bakery oven.

Ingredients

  • Dough: 500 g flour + yeast + sugar + salt + olive oil + warm water
  • Topping: ½ cup za'atar + ½ cup olive oil (or akkawi/cheese)
  • Cucumber + tomato + mint + olives to serve

Method

  1. Knead a soft dough 10 minutes; rise 1 hour until doubled.
  2. Divide into balls; rest 15 minutes; stretch into ovals.
  3. Dimple with fingertips; spread za'atar-oil paste edge to edge.
  4. Bake at maximum heat (250 °C+, steel/stone) 7–10 minutes.
  5. Serve immediately, warm, with vegetables and tea.

Chef tips

  • Maximum heat — a cool oven bakes crackers, not manakish.
  • Dimple the dough — undocked centers balloon.
  • Za'atar paste, not dry sprinkle — oil carries the flavor.
  • Eat warm — manakish stiffens as it cools.
