Banitsa

Bulgaria

Banitsa is the soul of Bulgarian baking — thin phyllo sheets layered with a loose, wet filling of crumbled sirene (Bulgaria's brined white cheese, saltier and crumblier than feta), beaten eggs and full-fat yogurt, then rolled into long coils and baked until deeply golden and shatteringly flaky. Eaten warm for breakfast with boza or ayran, it is also the centerpiece of Christmas and New Year's Eve, when lucky charms (kasmeti) are hidden inside. The soda-lightened yogurt-egg mixture puffs between the layers, giving authentic banitsa its custardy interior and crisp shell.

Ingredients

  • 1 pack (400–450 g) thin phyllo dough + 200 g melted butter (or sunflower oil)
  • 400 g Bulgarian sirene, coarsely crumbled (substitute: feta mixed with a little ricotta)
  • 4 large eggs + 300 ml full-fat plain yogurt + 1 tsp baking soda + 50 ml sparkling water

Method

  1. Whisk the eggs until frothy; stir in the yogurt and baking soda until smooth and bubbly.
  2. Crumble the sirene into the egg-yogurt mixture and stir gently — the filling should be loose and wet.
  3. Lay out phyllo sheets; brush each with melted butter and spread filling down one edge.
  4. Roll each sheet lengthwise into a long sausage, then coil the rolls snail-fashion into a buttered round pan.
  5. Brush the top generously with butter (and a splash of mineral water for extra lift).
  6. Bake at 180 °C (350 °F) for 35–45 minutes until puffed and deep gold; rest 10 minutes before slicing.

Chef tips

  • Sirene is non-negotiable for authentic flavor — plain feta alone tastes flat by comparison.
  • Keep phyllo covered with a damp towel while working; dried sheets crack and won't roll.
  • A loose, wet filling is correct — dry filling bakes dense instead of custardy.
  • Serve warm, never cold from the fridge — reheat in the oven to restore the crisp shell.
