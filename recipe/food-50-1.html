Sunday Roast

England

The Sunday roast is England's weekly ritual: roast beef (sirloin or topside) with giant Yorkshire puddings, goose-fat roast potatoes, roasted roots, greens, horseradish and rivers of gravy made from the pan drippings. Timing is the craft — everything lands hot at once — and the Yorkshire batter needs a screaming-hot tin and an oven door that stays shut. Rest the beef; never rest the potatoes.

Ingredients

  • 2 kg beef joint + horseradish + mustard + beef dripping
  • Yorkshires: 200 g flour + 4 eggs + 300 ml milk + salt
  • Potatoes + roots + greens + stock + flour for gravy

Method

  1. Bring beef to room temperature; rub with mustard, salt and dripping.
  2. Roast at 200 °C to 55–57 °C internal (medium-rare); rest 30+ minutes.
  3. Parboil potatoes; rough up; roast in hot dripping until glass-crisp.
  4. Heat Yorkshire tin with dripping until smoking; pour rested batter; bake 20–25 min, door shut.
  5. Deglaze the roasting pan; build gravy with stock.
  6. Carve; plate everything hot with gravy and horseradish.

Chef tips

  • Rest the beef 30 minutes — carving early floods the board.
  • Smoking tin, rested batter — flat Yorkshires mean cool fat.
  • Never open the oven — peeking collapses the puddings.
  • Gravy from drippings — granules are a different religion.
