Falafel

Palestine

Palestinian falafel is the green-centered, herb-explosive kind: dried chickpeas soaked (never cooked, never canned) and ground with abundant parsley, cilantro, mint, onion and garlic, spiced with cumin and coriander, then fried into dark-crisp fritters with emerald, fluffy hearts. Stuffed into pita with tahini, pickles, tomatoes and sometimes fries, it is the West Bank's everyday street food. The cardinal rule — soaked raw chickpeas only — is what separates shattering falafel from falling-apart mush.

Ingredients

  • 2 cups dried chickpeas, soaked 24 hours (never canned)
  • 2 bunches parsley + 1 cilantro + 1 mint + 1 onion + garlic
  • 2 tbsp cumin + 2 tbsp coriander + salt + pepper + ¾ tsp baking powder; oil at 175–180 °C

Method

  1. Soak chickpeas 24 hours; drain and pat very dry.
  2. Grind with herbs, onion and spices to a coarse, damp, packable meal — not a paste.
  3. Rest the mix 30+ minutes refrigerated; fold in baking powder just before frying.
  4. Shape with a scoop or wet hands into tight balls or patties.
  5. Fry at 175–180 °C until deep mahogany; drain; stuff into pita immediately.

Chef tips

  • Canned chickpeas guarantee failure — they disintegrate in the oil.
  • Coarse grind holds; over-processed paste turns dense and oily.
  • Fry a tester first — crumbling means the mix needs a rest or a spoon of flour.
  • Serve within minutes — falafel's crust softens as it stands.
