Tabbouleh

Syria

True Levantine tabbouleh is a parsley salad that happens to contain bulgur — not the other way round: mountains of finely chopped flat-leaf parsley and mint with diced tomato, scallion, lemon juice and olive oil, the bulgur a mere scattering that drinks the dressing. Light, lemony, herb-intense, scooped with romaine hearts or cabbage leaves, it is the fresh counterpoint to grilled meats across Syria and Lebanon. The chop must be fine and dry — wet parsley makes soup.

Ingredients

  • 3 large bunches flat-leaf parsley + 1 bunch mint, finely chopped
  • 3 tomatoes, finely diced + 4 scallions + ¼ cup fine bulgur
  • Lemon + olive oil + salt (+ cinnamon pinch, Aleppo-style)

Method

  1. Soak fine bulgur 10 minutes; squeeze very dry.
  2. Wash and thoroughly dry all herbs — wet herbs ruin tabbouleh.
  3. Chop parsley and mint very fine with a sharp knife (never a processor).
  4. Dice tomatoes fine; slice scallions thin.
  5. Toss everything with lemon, oil and salt; rest 15 minutes.
  6. Serve with romaine hearts for scooping.

Chef tips

  • Parsley leads, bulgur whispers — reverse the ratio and it's the wrong salad.
  • Dry herbs, sharp knife — bruised wet parsley tastes grassy.
  • Salt the tomatoes first and drain — their juice waters the salad.
  • Lemon boldly — timid lemon makes flat tabbouleh.
