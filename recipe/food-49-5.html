Hornado

Ecuador

Hornado is the Andes' whole-roast-pig celebration: pork leg or shoulder marinated in garlic, cumin, achiote and beer/chicha, slow-roasted for hours until the skin crackles into chicharrón and the meat pulls — served in markets by the plate with mote (hominy), llapingachos, curtido and agrio. The crackling is the prize: dry skin, salt, and a final blast of heat. Market hornado roasts whole pigs; home versions use leg or shoulder.

Ingredients

  • Pork leg/shoulder with skin + garlic + cumin + achiote + beer
  • Mote + llapingachos + curtido + agrio to serve

Method

  1. Score and salt the skin; marinate meat 12–24 hours.
  2. Roast low (150 °C) for 4–5 hours, basting.
  3. Dry the skin; blast at 220 °C+ until crackled.
  4. Rest 30 minutes.
  5. Serve with mote, llapingachos, curtido and agrio.

Chef tips

  • Dry skin crackles — pat dry and salt ahead.
  • Low then blast — slow tenderness, fierce finish.
  • Rest well — juices redistribute.
  • Agrio cuts the fat — the sharp salsa is essential.
