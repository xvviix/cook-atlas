Mekitsa

Bulgaria

Mekitsa (plural mekitsi/mekitzi) are Bulgaria's puffy breakfast fritters — a soft yogurt dough, stretched thin by hand and deep-fried until golden and blistered, then eaten hot with powdered sugar, jam or sirene cheese. The yogurt-baking soda reaction gives them their characteristic airy chew without a long rise, making them a fast morning treat. Served alongside banitsa at every Bulgarian bakery breakfast, they are best eaten within minutes of frying.

Ingredients

  • 3 cups flour + 1 cup yogurt + 2 eggs + 1 tsp baking soda + pinch of salt + ½ cup water
  • Oil for deep-frying + powdered sugar / jam / sirene to serve

Method

  1. Mix flour with soda and salt; add eggs, yogurt and water; knead to a soft, slightly sticky dough.
  2. Rest covered 20–40 minutes to relax the gluten.
  3. With oiled hands, pull off balls and stretch each thin, pizza-style (thin centers puff best).
  4. Fry in 175–180 °C oil until puffed and golden on both sides.
  5. Drain briefly; dust with sugar or top with jam/cheese; serve immediately.

Chef tips

  • Stretch thin — thick mekitsi stay doughy inside.
  • Oil at 175–180 °C — cool oil soaks in grease; smoking oil burns raw centers.
  • Oiled hands, not floured — flour burns in the fryer.
  • Serve immediately — mekitsi deflate and toughen as they cool.
