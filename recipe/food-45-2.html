Fattoush

Syria

Fattoush is the Levant's bread salad: crisp romaine, cucumber, tomato, radish, purslane and a tangle of fresh mint and parsley, dressed in lemon-olive oil with defining sumac (and often pomegranate molasses), then tossed with toasted or fried pita chips at the very last second. The pita must stay crunchy against the juicy vegetables — timing is the recipe. Peasant-born (using stale bread), now the brightest starter on every Syrian table.

Ingredients

  • Romaine + cucumber + tomatoes + radishes + spring onions + purslane
  • Mint + parsley, generously + sumac + lemon + olive oil (+ pomegranate molasses)
  • 2 pitas, toasted/fried into chips

Method

  1. Toast or fry pita until deeply crisp; break into bite-size shards.
  2. Chop all vegetables chunky; tear the herbs.
  3. Whisk lemon, oil, sumac, salt and molasses into a brick-red dressing.
  4. Toss vegetables and herbs with dressing.
  5. Add pita chips; toss once; serve immediately.

Chef tips

  • Pita last, always — early pita turns to mush.
  • Sumac generously — it's the soul, not a garnish.
  • Chunky chop — fine dice wilts into slaw.
  • Serve at once — fattoush waits for no one.
