Dizi / Abgoosht

Iran

Abgoosht ('meat-water') — cooked and served in stone dizi crocks — is Iran's ancient working-man's stew: lamb, chickpeas, white beans, potatoes, tomatoes and dried lime simmered for hours, then ritually separated — the broth eaten as soup with torn bread (tilit), the solids mashed (koobideh, no relation) with pickles and onion. The two-course ritual from one pot is the soul of the dish, still performed in traditional teahouses.

Ingredients

  • 700 g lamb (shank/neck) + bones if possible
  • Chickpeas + white beans, soaked + potatoes + tomatoes + onion
  • Dried limes + turmeric + cinnamon + salt; bread + pickles + onion to serve

Method

  1. Simmer lamb, beans, onion, turmeric and limes 2–2.5 hours until collapsing.
  2. Add potatoes and tomatoes for the final 45 minutes.
  3. Lift out solids; strain the broth; season both.
  4. First course: tear bread into the broth; eat as soup.
  5. Second course: mash the solids coarse (goosht-koob); eat with pickles and onion.

Chef tips

  • Two courses from one pot — serve the ritual, not just the food.
  • Mash coarse — baby-food smooth is wrong.
  • Dried lime in the broth — its sourness cuts the richness.
  • Bones in the pot — they make the broth silky.
