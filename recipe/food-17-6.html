Acarajé

Brazil

Bahia's Afro-Brazilian street icon: black-eyed-pea fritters fried in dendê, split and stuffed with vatapá (cashew-shrimp-coconut cream), caruru (okra), dried shrimp, salad and wicked chile. Sold by baianas in white lace — a whole meal in one fist.

Ingredients

  • Fritters: 500 g black-eyed peas (soaked, peeled) + onion, blended; salt; dendê for frying
  • Vatapá: dried shrimp + peanuts + cashews + coconut milk + bread + dendê + ginger + chile
  • Caruru: okra + dried shrimp + peanuts + dendê
  • Fillings: vatapá + caruru + dried shrimp + tomato salad + pimenta

Method

  1. Peel the soaked peas (rub and rinse repeatedly); blend with onion to a thick batter; whip airy.
  2. Fry spoonfuls in 175 °C dendê 4–5 minutes to deep gold; drain.
  3. Blend and simmer the vatapá to a thick, glossy cream; stew the caruru.
  4. Split each acarajé; stuff generously with vatapá, caruru, shrimp and salad.
  5. Ask 'quente ou fria?' — hot means chile. Serve immediately.

Chef tips

  • Peeling the peas is tedious and mandatory — skins ruin the texture.
  • Dendê fries AND flavors — substituting oil loses the soul.
  • Serve seconds from the fryer; acarajé waits for no one.
