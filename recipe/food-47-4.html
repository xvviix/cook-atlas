Patat

Netherlands

Dutch patat means thick-cut chips, twice-fried (parboiled or first-fried, rested, then finished crisp), served in a paper cone (puntzak) with the sauce ladled over — mayo (fritesaus), curry ketchup, or the holy trinity of patatje oorlog: peanut satay sauce, mayo and raw onion. Floury Agria or Santé potatoes, beef tallow or groundnut oil, and a proper double fry give the glass-crisp shell and fluffy heart of a real frietkot. Sauce on top, never on the side.

Ingredients

  • Floury potatoes (Agria/Santé) + tallow or groundnut oil + salt
  • Sauces: frites-mayo + satay sauce + curry ketchup + raw onion

Method

  1. Cut thick (12–15 mm); rinse and dry thoroughly.
  2. First fry at 150 °C until soft and pale; drain and rest.
  3. Second fry at 180 °C until glass-crisp and gold.
  4. Salt immediately; cone them up.
  5. Ladle sauce over (oorlog = satay + mayo + onion); eat with a fork.

Chef tips

  • Floury potatoes — waxy ones fry leathery.
  • Twice-fried — single-fried chips steam limp.
  • Dry before frying — water is the enemy of crisp.
  • Sauce over, eaten fast — patat waits for no one.
