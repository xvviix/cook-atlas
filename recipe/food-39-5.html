Misir Wat

Ethiopia

Misir wat — spiced red lentils — is the jewel of the fasting platter: red lentils simmered with slowly softened onion, garlic, ginger, berbere and tomato into a thick, brick-red, creamy stew finished with mekelesha (Ethiopian finishing spice). Naturally vegan and one-pot, it is weeknight-easy yet complex enough for guests. The lentils should collapse entirely into silk — simmer until no whole lentil survives.

Ingredients

  • 1.5 cups red lentils, rinsed
  • 1 large onion + 4 garlic cloves + ginger + 2 tbsp berbere + tomato
  • Oil + paprika + mekelesha + salt; injera or rice to serve

Method

  1. Soften onion, garlic and ginger low and slow, 15–20 minutes.
  2. Bloom berbere and paprika in the oil to a dark paste.
  3. Add tomato; cook 2 minutes; add lentils and water.
  4. Simmer 20–25 minutes until the lentils fully collapse.
  5. Mash lightly; adjust thickness; finish with mekelesha and salt.
  6. Serve hot with injera — it thickens as it cools.

Chef tips

  • Collapse the lentils fully — al dente lentils aren't misir wat.
  • Bloom the berbere in oil — raw berbere tastes harsh.
  • Low, patient onion-cooking builds the sweetness.
  • Leftovers thicken — loosen with hot water when reheating.
