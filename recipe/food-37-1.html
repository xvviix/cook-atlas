Maqluba

Palestine

Maqluba ('upside-down') is Palestine's showpiece Friday dish: chicken, fried cauliflower, eggplant and potatoes layered under spiced basmati in a deep pot, cooked in rich broth, rested, then flipped dramatically tableside to reveal a towering molded cake of rice. The flip is theater and test in one — a clean release means a perfect maqluba. Served with yogurt-cucumber salad and Arab salad, it feeds a crowd from a single pot.

Ingredients

  • 3 chicken leg quarters + 2 cups basmati, soaked
  • Cauliflower + eggplant + potatoes, fried; tomatoes for lining
  • Baharat + turmeric + cardamom + bay + stock; toasted almonds + parsley

Method

  1. Simmer chicken with cardamom and bay 25 minutes; reserve the broth.
  2. Fry the vegetables in batches until golden; drain well.
  3. Line the pot with tomato slices (they prevent sticking and look beautiful flipped).
  4. Layer: vegetables, chicken, then rice; season layers with baharat and turmeric.
  5. Pour over hot broth to just cover the rice; simmer covered 25–30 minutes.
  6. Rest off heat 15 minutes; invert onto a platter in one confident motion.

Chef tips

  • Rest before flipping — skipping the rest guarantees collapse.
  • Fry vegetables golden — pale vegetables steam to mush in the pot.
  • Broth just covering the rice — too much makes porridge.
  • One confident flip — hesitation sticks half the rice to the pot.
