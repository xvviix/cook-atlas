Umm Ali

Egypt

Umm Ali ('Ali's mother') is Egypt's legendary bread pudding, with a backstory of medieval palace intrigue: layers of baked puff pastry (or ruqaq) shattered with toasted nuts, raisins and coconut, drowned in hot sweetened milk and cream, then baked until puffed and bronzed. Served bubbling hot, it is the dessert of gatherings — rich, milky, nutty comfort. The pastry must be baked first: raw pastry turns gummy instead of slipperily layered.

Ingredients

  • 1 sheet puff pastry, baked golden + broken
  • 1 liter hot milk + 200 ml cream + 150 g sugar + vanilla/cinnamon
  • Mixed toasted nuts + raisins + shredded coconut; extra cream to finish

Method

  1. Bake the puff pastry until deeply golden; break into shards.
  2. Layer shards with nuts, raisins and coconut in a deep baking dish.
  3. Heat milk with cream, sugar and vanilla to steaming.
  4. Pour over slowly, soaking every piece; dot with cream.
  5. Bake at 180 °C for 20–25 minutes until puffed and bronzed.
  6. Serve immediately, bubbling, with extra cream if desired.

Chef tips

  • Bake the pastry first — raw pastry makes glue, not pudding.
  • Hot milk over the layers — cold milk soaks unevenly.
  • Bronze the top — pale umm ali looks and tastes unfinished.
  • Serve bubbling — it settles and firms as it cools.
