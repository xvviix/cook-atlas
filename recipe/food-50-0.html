Fish and Chips

England

England's national dish: thick cod or haddock fillets in a bubbly beer batter (cold beer + flour + baking powder, mixed lumpy), fried at 180–190 °C to a crisp golden armor around steaming flaky fish — with twice-cooked chips, salt, malt vinegar, and mushy peas or tartare. The batter must be cold and the oil hot; chip-shop beef dripping is the traditional (and best) frying medium. Eat from paper, with vinegar, preferably by the sea.

Ingredients

  • 4 thick cod/haddock fillets + flour for dusting
  • Batter: 250 g flour + baking powder + 330 ml cold beer + salt
  • Chips: floury potatoes, twice-fried + malt vinegar + salt + mushy peas

Method

  1. Cut chips thick; rinse, dry; first fry at 150 °C; rest.
  2. Heat oil to 180–190 °C; mix batter cold and lumpy at the last minute.
  3. Dust fish in flour; dip in batter; lower away from you.
  4. Fry 6–8 minutes until deep gold and crisp; drain on a rack.
  5. Second-fry chips until crisp; salt everything.
  6. Serve immediately with vinegar and mushy peas.

Chef tips

  • Cold batter, hot oil — the thermal shock makes crispness.
  • Lumpy batter — overmixing develops gluten and heaviness.
  • Fry fish last — it waits for nothing.
  • Drain on a rack — paper steams the crust soft.
