Moussaka

Greece

Greece's national baked casserole: layers of fried eggplant (and usually potato) with a cinnamon-scented lamb or beef tomato sauce, crowned with a thick egg-enriched béchamel that bakes to a golden crust. Served warm in squares, traditionally with a Horiatiki salad.

Ingredients

  • 2 large eggplants, sliced ~6 mm; 2 potatoes, sliced
  • 500 g minced lamb or beef
  • 1 onion + 2 garlic cloves, chopped
  • 400 g crushed tomatoes + 2 tbsp tomato paste
  • 120 ml red wine
  • 1 tsp cinnamon + pinch of allspice, oregano, bay leaf
  • Béchamel: 60 g butter, 60 g flour, 700 ml milk, 2 egg yolks, nutmeg, 50 g grated Kefalotyri/Parmesan
  • Olive oil, salt, pepper; breadcrumbs for the dish

Method

  1. Salt the eggplant slices, rest 30 minutes, pat dry, then fry or brush with oil and roast until golden; fry/roast the potato slices too.
  2. Brown the mince with onion and garlic, add wine, then tomatoes, paste and spices; simmer 25–30 minutes until thick.
  3. Make the béchamel: roux of butter + flour, whisk in hot milk until thick, season with salt and nutmeg, cool slightly, then whisk in yolks and half the cheese.
  4. Layer in a breadcrumb-dusted dish: potatoes, half the eggplant, all the meat sauce, remaining eggplant; pour over the béchamel and sprinkle cheese.
  5. Bake at 180 °C (350 °F) 40–50 minutes until deep golden and set; rest 20–30 minutes before cutting so slices hold.

Chef tips

  • Resting is essential — cut too soon and the layers slide apart.
  • Roasting instead of deep-frying the vegetables gives a lighter, less oily result.
  • Moussaka reheats beautifully; many consider it better on day two.
