Knödel

Austria

Semmelknödel — bread dumplings — are the backbone of Austrian plates: stale Semmel rolls cubed and soaked in warm milk with egg, parsley and sautéed onion, formed into fist-sized balls and poached until they float. They exist to drink gravy: goulash, mushroom sauce, roast juices. The test of a good knödel is a light, even crumb that holds together yet yields to the fork — achieved by day-old bread, a rested mixture and gently simmering (never boiling) water.

Ingredients

  • 250 g stale Semmel/rolls, cubed + 180 ml warm milk + 2 eggs
  • 1 onion + parsley + butter + salt + pepper + nutmeg (+ bacon optional)

Method

  1. Sauté onion (and bacon) in butter; pour warm milk over bread cubes; rest 15 minutes.
  2. Mix in eggs, onion, parsley and seasonings; rest 15–30 minutes more.
  3. With wet hands, form firm fist-sized balls; chill 30 minutes if soft.
  4. Poach in barely simmering salted water 15–20 minutes until they float plus 5.
  5. Test one first — if it falls apart, bind the mix with breadcrumbs.
  6. Serve with goulash, mushroom gravy or roast with lashings of sauce.

Chef tips

  • Stale bread is essential — fresh bread turns to mush.
  • Poach, don't boil — agitation shatters dumplings.
  • Always test-cook one — rescuing the batch with crumbs saves dinner.
  • Wet hands shape cleanly — dry hands tear the surface.
