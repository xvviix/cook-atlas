Mahjouba

Algeria

Mahjouba (mhajeb/mhadjeb — same family, see food-46-7) is Algeria's stuffed griddle flatbread: elastic semolina dough stretched paper-thin by oiled hands, spread with a jammy filling of slow-cooked onion, tomato, peppers and harissa, folded into a square parcel and griddled until blistered and crisp. Street food across Algiers, eaten folded in paper, hot enough to burn fingers. The dough must rest long enough to stretch without tearing — elasticity is everything.

Ingredients

  • Dough: 500 g fine semolina + salt + water + oil for stretching
  • Filling: 3 onions + 3 tomatoes + peppers + garlic + harissa + cumin
  • Oil for the griddle

Method

  1. Knead semolina dough 10 minutes; rest oiled 1+ hour until very elastic.
  2. Cook filling low until jammy and almost dry; cool.
  3. Stretch each ball paper-thin on an oiled surface.
  4. Spread filling; fold envelope-style into a square.
  5. Griddle in light oil until blistered and gold both sides.
  6. Serve immediately, folded in paper.

Chef tips

  • Long rest, elastic dough — impatient dough tears.
  • Filling nearly dry — wet filling steams the parcel soggy.
  • Stretch, don't roll — hands give the paper-thin pull.
  • Eat immediately — crispness fades in minutes.
