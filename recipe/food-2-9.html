Feta

Greece

A PDO brined cheese made only in mainland Greece and Lesbos from sheep's milk (up to 30% goat's milk allowed) — 'feta' means 'slice'. White, rindless blocks are dry-salted, then ripened in brine at least 2 months, developing a creamy-crumbly texture and bright salty tang. This card covers traditional production plus selection and use.

Ingredients

  • Sheep's milk (up to 30% goat's milk)
  • Starter cultures + rennet
  • Coarse sea salt; brine (~7–8% salt)
  • To serve: olive oil, oregano, bread, olives, honey (optional)

Method

  1. Pasteurized (or raw, artisanal) milk is cultured and set with rennet at ~30 °C; the curd is cut and drained without pressing in perforated molds.
  2. The mass is cut into blocks, dry-salted and left ~2 days to firm and acidify.
  3. Blocks are submerged in brine and ripened in two stages — first in temperature-controlled rooms, then cold — for a minimum of 2 months.
  4. At home: buy blocks stored in brine (not pre-crumbled dry packs) from sheep's milk for authentic flavor.
  5. Rinse briefly if very salty, bring to room temperature, and serve as a slab with olive oil and oregano — or baked (saganaki-style) with honey and sesame.

Chef tips

  • Real feta lists sheep's (and maybe goat's) milk — cow's-milk 'feta-style' cheese is a different product.
  • The longer in brine, the firmer and sharper; young feta is mild and creamy.
  • Keep leftovers submerged in its brine in the fridge; it lasts for weeks.
