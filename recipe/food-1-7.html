Parmigiano Reggiano

Italy

A PDO hard cheese made since the Middle Ages in Parma, Reggio Emilia, Modena, Bologna-west-of-Reno and Mantua-east-of-Po, from just three ingredients: milk, salt and rennet. It is not 'made' at home — this card describes the traditional production and how to choose, keep and use it. Minimum aging is 12 months (the longest minimum of any PDO cheese).

Ingredients

  • Partially skimmed cow's milk (evening + morning milkings)
  • Natural whey starter
  • Calf rennet
  • Sea salt (brine bath)
  • To serve at home: the cheese wedge, balsamic vinegar / honey / walnuts (optional)

Method

  1. Morning and evening milk are combined in copper cauldrons, warmed, and coagulated with whey starter and rennet.
  2. The curd is broken into tiny granules with a balloon whisk (spino), then cooked so the granules sink and fuse into one mass.
  3. The mass is split into two wheels, molded in a fascera 2–3 days, and stamped with the dotted 'Parmigiano Reggiano' mark, dairy and date.
  4. Wheels brine in salt water ~20 days, then age on wooden shelves (scalere) at ~18 °C / 82% humidity for a minimum of 12 months.
  5. At 12 months Consortium experts inspect each wheel; only passing wheels are fire-branded and may age on to 24, 30, 36+ months.
  6. At home: buy wedges with the dotted rind mark; serve at room temperature in shards (never cubes cut with a knife — crack with an almond knife).

Chef tips

  • Age guide: 12–18 mo — milky, delicate, good for shaving; 24 mo — balanced; 30–36+ mo — crumbly, nutty, with tyrosine crystals, best with balsamic and honey.
  • Real Parmigiano always shows the pin-dot 'Parmigiano Reggiano' rind stamp; 'Parmesan' alone is not the PDO cheese.
  • Wrap in wax paper then foil; never freeze; bring to room temperature before serving.
