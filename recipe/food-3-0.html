Ceviche

Peru

Peru's national dish: cubes of very fresh sea fish (sea bass, sole, snapper) briefly marinated in lime juice with ají peppers, red onion and cilantro. The marinade, leche de tigre ('tiger's milk'), cures the fish in minutes and is prized as a drink or hangover cure. Served with choclo corn, camote sweet potato and cancha toasted corn.

Ingredients

  • 500 g sushi-grade firm white sea fish, cut in ~2 cm cubes
  • 8–10 limes, freshly squeezed (~250 ml juice)
  • ½ red onion, thinly sliced
  • 1 ají limo or ají amarillo (or jalapeño), minced; 1 tbsp ají amarillo paste (optional)
  • Leche de tigre extras: 1 garlic clove, celery stalk, cilantro stems, ginger, fish trimmings, salt
  • Cilantro leaves, salt
  • To serve: choclo corn, boiled camote, cancha corn nuts

Method

  1. Soak the sliced onion in ice water 20–30 minutes to soften its bite; drain.
  2. Blend lime juice with garlic, celery, cilantro stems, ginger, a little fish trim and salt; strain — this is the leche de tigre.
  3. Season the fish cubes with salt, toss with onion, ají and cilantro, then pour over the leche de tigre.
  4. Marinate only 5–10 minutes — the fish should be just opaque at the edges, still silky inside.
  5. Serve immediately in bowls with corn, sweet potato and cancha, extra leche de tigre on the side.

Chef tips

  • Fish must be impeccably fresh — ceviche is 'cooked' by acid, not heat, so quality is safety.
  • Lime juice must be fresh-squeezed; bottled juice tastes flat and over-acidic.
  • Over-marinating turns the fish chalky — Peruvian ceviche is made to order, never ahead.
