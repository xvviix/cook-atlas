Injera

Ethiopia

Injera is Ethiopia's sour, spongy, crepe-like flatbread — simultaneously plate, utensil and food — made from fermented teff flour batter poured onto a scorching mitad griddle, where it blisters into a landscape of tiny eyes (ayn). The 2–3 day fermentation builds its signature tang and open crumb; it is cooked on one side only, under a lid, never flipped. Eaten daily across Ethiopia and Eritrea, gluten-free when pure teff, it is the foundation every wat and tibs is built on.

Ingredients

  • 1.5 cups teff flour (+ 1 cup wheat/barley flour optional, for elasticity)
  • 3–4 cups warm water + pinch of yeast or sourdough starter (optional accelerator)
  • Salt to taste + oil for the pan

Method

  1. Whisk flour with warm water to a thin crêpe-batter consistency; cover loosely.
  2. Ferment at room temperature 2–3 days until sour-smelling and bubbly.
  3. Pour off any dark surface liquid; stir; thin to pourable if needed; salt.
  4. Heat a nonstick skillet/mitad until very hot; lightly oil and wipe out.
  5. Pour a thin spiral from edge to center; cover immediately; steam 1–2 minutes.
  6. Slide out without flipping; cool on cloth; stack when fully cool.

Chef tips

  • Fermentation is the flavor — under-fermented injera tastes flat.
  • Never flip — the lid's steam cooks the top while the bottom crisps.
  • Thin pour, hot pan — thick batter makes gummy, pancake-like injera.
  • Cool before stacking — hot injera steams itself sticky.
