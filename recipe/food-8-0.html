Peking Duck

China

Beijing's lacquered icon: a whole duck scalded, glazed with maltose, air-dried 24–48 hours and roasted to glass-crisp, mahogany skin. Carved tableside and wrapped in pancakes with hoisin, scallion and cucumber — the meat returns as a second course.

Ingredients

  • 1 whole duck (~2 kg)
  • Glaze: maltose/honey + Shaoxing + light/dark soy
  • Five-spice + ginger for the cavity; salt
  • 16–20 mandarin pancakes; hoisin; scallions; cucumber sticks

Method

  1. Clean and pat dry; ladle boiling water over the skin to tighten it.
  2. Brush all over with the glaze; hang or rack uncovered in the fridge 24–48 hours until papery dry.
  3. Roast vertical on a rack at 175–200 °C 1.5–2 hours, turning for even lacquer; the skin should tap hollow.
  4. Rest 15 minutes; carve skin with a little meat in front of guests.
  5. Wrap: pancake + hoisin + skin + scallion + cucumber. Stir-fry the remaining meat or simmer the carcass into soup.

Chef tips

  • Dry skin is the whole game — any moisture steams instead of crisps.
  • The scald-then-dry sequence can't be shortcut.
  • Two courses from one bird is the tradition — plan the second.
