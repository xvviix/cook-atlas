Molokhia

Egypt

Molokhia — minced jute mallow simmered in chicken or rabbit broth — is Egypt's most beloved green stew, famous for its silky, okra-like texture and the dramatic ta'leya: garlic and ground coriander sizzled in samneh until golden, then poured hissing into the pot. Never boiled after the leaves go in (boiling turns it slimy-stringy and dulls the green), it is served over rice with chicken and lemon. Egyptians swear by the sizzle — some even gasp (sheh'a) as it hits.

Ingredients

  • 500 g minced molokhia (fresh or frozen, thawed)
  • 1 liter rich chicken broth + salt
  • Ta'leya: 6 garlic cloves + 1.5 tbsp ground coriander + 2 tbsp samneh

Method

  1. Bring the broth to steaming — hot but explicitly not boiling.
  2. Whisk in the molokhia; heat through gently without ever boiling.
  3. Meanwhile, sizzle garlic in samneh until pale gold; add coriander 30 seconds more.
  4. Pour the ta'leya hissing into the pot; stir once.
  5. Serve immediately over rice with chicken, lemon and pickles.

Chef tips

  • Never boil molokhia — boiling ruins texture and color.
  • Frozen minced molokhia is excellent — no shame in it.
  • Golden garlic, not brown — burnt garlic turns the pot bitter.
  • Serve at once — molokhia waits for no one.
