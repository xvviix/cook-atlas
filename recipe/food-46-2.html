Chtitha

Algeria

Chtitha (chtitha djedj with chicken, chtitha lhem with meat) is Algeria's dersa-driven stew: chicken or lamb simmered with chickpeas in a thick, brick-red sauce built from pounded garlic, paprika, cumin, caraway and tomato — the dersa paste fried first until it stains the oil. A Ramadan-table staple, punchy with garlic and warming spices, mopped with bread. The dersa fry is the soul: raw-garlic sauce tastes harsh; fried dersa tastes deep.

Ingredients

  • 1 kg chicken pieces or lamb + 2 cups chickpeas, soaked
  • Dersa: 1 head garlic + paprika + cumin + caraway + salt + oil
  • Tomato + harissa + parsley + lemon to serve

Method

  1. Pound garlic with spices and salt into a thick dersa paste.
  2. Fry the dersa in oil 2–3 minutes until dark and fragrant.
  3. Add meat; brown in the spiced oil.
  4. Add tomato, chickpeas and water; simmer 40–50 minutes.
  5. Reduce to a thick, clinging sauce; shower with parsley.
  6. Serve with bread and lemon.

Chef tips

  • Fry the dersa first — raw garlic paste tastes harsh.
  • Reduce properly — thin chtitha is just soup.
  • Chickpeas tender, not mush — they should hold shape.
  • Lemon at the end lifts the richness.
