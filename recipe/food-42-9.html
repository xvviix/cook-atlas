Kashk-e Bademjan

Iran

Kashk-e bademjan is Persia's silkiest eggplant dip: eggplants roasted or fried to collapse, mashed with garlic and onion, enriched with kashk (salty fermented whey) and crowned with caramelized onions, sizzled mint and walnuts. Served warm with sangak bread as appetizer or light meal, it balances smoky, creamy, tangy and sweet in every scoop. Roasting over flame adds smoke that frying can't match.

Ingredients

  • 3 large eggplants + 1 cup kashk + garlic + 2 onions
  • Toppings: caramelized onion + mint sizzled in oil + walnuts
  • Turmeric + salt + pepper + sangak/flatbread to serve

Method

  1. Char eggplants over flame (or roast hot) until fully collapsed; scoop flesh.
  2. Caramelize onions slowly; fry garlic golden.
  3. Mash eggplant with garlic, onion and turmeric; enrich with kashk.
  4. Warm through gently — never boil after kashk joins.
  5. Plate; crown with onions, mint oil and walnuts; serve with bread.

Chef tips

  • Flame-char for smoke — plain-roasted eggplant tastes flat beside it.
  • Collapse fully — undercooked eggplant mashes lumpy.
  • Kashk off the boil — boiling splits and dulls it.
  • Warm serving — cold mutes the silkiness.
