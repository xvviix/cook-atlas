Lomo Saltado

Peru

The flagship of chifa (Peruvian-Chinese) cooking: strips of beef sirloin wok-tossed at fierce heat with red onion, tomato, ají amarillo, soy sauce and vinegar, then mixed with crispy fries and served over rice. The smoky breath of the wok (wok hei) defines it.

Ingredients

  • 600 g beef sirloin or tenderloin, sliced in strips
  • 3 tbsp soy sauce + 2 tbsp red wine vinegar
  • 1 tsp sugar (optional)
  • 1 large red onion, thick wedges; 3 tomatoes, wedges
  • 1 ají amarillo (or bell + chili), sliced; 3 garlic cloves + ginger, minced
  • 400 g potatoes as thick fries (or frozen fries)
  • Cilantro, salt, pepper; oil for wok
  • Steamed white rice to serve

Method

  1. Marinate the beef in 1 tbsp soy + sugar 30–60 minutes; fry or bake the fries until crisp and keep hot.
  2. Heat the wok until smoking with a film of oil; sear the beef in small batches 1–2 minutes — never crowd — and set aside.
  3. Flash-fry onion, ají, garlic and ginger 2–3 minutes; add tomatoes until just softening at the edges.
  4. Return the beef with remaining soy and vinegar; toss hard 1 minute so the sauce glazes everything.
  5. Fold in the fries (or lay them on top so they stay crisp), shower with cilantro, and serve over rice.

Chef tips

  • Small batches + maximum heat = sear, not stew; a crowded wok boils the meat grey.
  • Fries go in at the very end so they keep their crunch against the juicy stir-fry.
  • Pisco or oyster sauce in the marinade is a popular chifa touch.
