Pouding Chômeur

Canada

'Unemployed man's pudding' — Depression-era Quebec genius: plain vanilla cake batter that bakes upside-down beneath boiling maple-toffee syrup, emerging with gooey caramel depths and a tender golden top. The syrup sinks as the cake rises, so each spoonful runs from cakey to molten. Serve warm with cream or ice cream; cold pouding chômeur, while still good, hides its magic.

Ingredients

  • Cake: flour + brown sugar + butter + egg + milk + baking powder + vanilla
  • Syrup: maple syrup (or brown sugar) + cream/butter + water, boiled

Method

  1. Whisk a simple cake batter; spread in a buttered deep dish.
  2. Boil the syrup ingredients together until smooth and bubbling.
  3. Pour the boiling syrup gently over the raw batter (yes, really).
  4. Bake at 175 °C for 30–35 minutes until risen and golden.
  5. Rest 5 minutes; scoop warm with cream.

Chef tips

  • Boiling syrup over raw batter — the inversion is the recipe.
  • Deep dish only — the syrup bubbles dramatically.
  • Maple syrup for the classic; brown sugar for the budget original.
  • Serve warm — the molten layer sets as it cools.
