Wiener Schnitzel

Austria

Austria's national dish is protected by culinary law in spirit: a true Wiener Schnitzel is made only from veal (pork becomes Wiener Art), pounded thin, breaded in flour-egg-fresh breadcrumbs and fried in abundant fat until the coating billows into golden waves (soufflieren). Served with lemon, parsley potatoes or cranberries — never sauce on top — it is the benchmark of Viennese cooking. The secrets are a loose, unpressed crumb coat, constantly swirled hot fat, and serving within seconds of the pan.

Ingredients

  • 4 veal cutlets (topside), pounded to 4 mm
  • Flour + 2 beaten eggs + 2 cups fresh breadcrumbs (not panko)
  • Clarified butter/lard + oil for shallow-frying + lemon + parsley

Method

  1. Pound cutlets evenly between film; season with salt and pepper.
  2. Dredge lightly: flour (shake off), egg, then loose breadcrumbs — never press.
  3. Heat 1 cm fat to 170–175 °C; the schnitzel must swim, not sit.
  4. Fry ~2 minutes per side, swirling the pan so fat washes over the top.
  5. Drain on a rack (not paper, which steams the crust); lemon over; serve instantly.

Chef tips

  • Never press the crumbs — a loose coat billows; a pressed one sticks flat.
  • Fresh breadcrumbs puff; dry crumbs or panko give a different, denser crust.
  • Veal only for Wiener Schnitzel — pork is Schnitzel Wiener Art.
  • No sauce on the schnitzel — it murders the crispness.
