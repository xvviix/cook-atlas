Pastel de Belém

Portugal

The original custard tart from the Fábrica de Pastéis de Belém (1837), baked to a secret convent recipe from the Jerónimos Monastery: crisp laminated shells filled with lemon-cinnamon egg custard, blistered under fierce heat. Served warm, dusted with cinnamon and powdered sugar. (Generic versions are called pastéis de nata — see that card.)

Ingredients

  • 300–450 g puff pastry (rolled into a log, sliced)
  • 250 g sugar + 160 ml water (syrup)
  • 300 ml whole milk + 30 g flour + pinch of salt
  • 6 egg yolks + 1 tsp vanilla
  • 1 cinnamon stick + wide strips of lemon peel
  • Cinnamon + powdered sugar to dust

Method

  1. Simmer sugar, water, cinnamon and lemon peel 3 minutes into a syrup; set aside.
  2. Whisk flour and salt into hot milk until thick; temper in the yolks and vanilla off the heat.
  3. Stream the warm syrup into the custard, whisking constantly; strain — the custard will be thin, which is correct.
  4. Press pastry slices thin into buttered molds (thicker at the rim); chill while the oven heats to maximum (250–300 °C).
  5. Fill shells ¾ full and bake 12–18 minutes until the pastry is golden and the custard is burnished with dark spots.
  6. Cool a few minutes and serve warm with cinnamon and sugar.

Chef tips

  • Maximum heat is the secret — a timid oven gives pale, soggy tarts.
  • The custard must never boil in the molds; pull the tarts before they puff and burst.
  • Eat within minutes of baking: Belém's rule is 'served warm'.
