Shiro Wat

Ethiopia

Shiro wat is Ethiopia's humble, beloved chickpea-flour stew — the fasting-season staple: roasted shiro powder (chickpea or pea flour milled with berbere and spices) whisked into a garlicky onion-tomato base and simmered to a smooth, porridge-thick, deeply savory stew. Cheap, fast, vegan and protein-rich, it appears on every beyaynetu platter and in every home during the ~200 Orthodox fasting days. Lumps are the enemy — the powder must go in gradually, whisking constantly.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup shiro powder (or chickpea flour + berbere + cumin + garlic powder)
  • 1 onion + 2 garlic cloves + 1 tomato + 120 ml oil or niter kibbeh
  • 4–5 cups hot water + salt + jalapeño to garnish

Method

  1. Sweat the onion slowly in oil until soft and sweet, adding water to prevent browning.
  2. Add tomato and garlic; cook down to a thick base.
  3. Add hot water and bring to a boil.
  4. Whisk in shiro powder gradually, a spoonful at a time, stirring constantly.
  5. Simmer 10–15 minutes, stirring often, until thick and glossy.
  6. Season with berbere and salt; serve bubbling on injera.

Chef tips

  • Gradual powder + constant stirring — dumping makes irreversible lumps.
  • Dry-roast the chickpea flour first for deeper, nuttier flavor.
  • Thin with hot water, never cold — cold water seizes the starch.
  • Shiro thickens as it stands — serve slightly looser than you want.
