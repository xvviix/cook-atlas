Picarones

Peru

Peru's beloved street doughnuts: yeasted rings of squash and sweet-potato dough perfumed with anise, fried crisp outside and fluffy inside, then drowned in chancaca syrup (raw cane sugar with cinnamon, clove, orange and fig leaf). Served piping hot.

Ingredients

  • Dough: 500 g flour, 250 g each cooked squash + sweet potato (mashed)
  • 2 tbsp active dry yeast + 1 tbsp sugar, activated in warm cooking water
  • 2 tsp aniseed; 1 cup warm squash/potato water; oil for deep-frying
  • Chancaca syrup: 2 chancaca/panela cones (or dark brown sugar + molasses)
  • Cinnamon, cloves, star anise, orange peel, fig leaf, pineapple peel (optional)

Method

  1. Simmer squash and sweet potato with aniseed; reserve the cooking water; mash smooth and cool to lukewarm.
  2. Activate the yeast in a cup of the warm water with sugar until foamy (~10 minutes).
  3. Knead mash + yeast + flour + water into a soft, silky, slightly sticky dough; proof covered ~1 hour until doubled.
  4. Simmer chancaca with spices and peels until thick and syrupy; strain and keep warm.
  5. With wet/oiled hands pull off dough pieces, form rings, and fry at 175 °C until deep golden and crisp.
  6. Drain briefly and serve immediately, generously drenched in warm chancaca.

Chef tips

  • Wet hands are the street-vendor trick — the sticky dough won't cling.
  • Oil at 175 °C: cooler oil = greasy rings; hotter = raw centers.
  • Picarones wait for no one — fry and serve in the same minutes.
