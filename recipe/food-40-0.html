Poutine

Canada

Quebec's gift to late-night humanity: twice-cooked fries, fresh cheese curds that squeak against the teeth, and hot brown gravy that half-melts the curds into glorious strings — assembled only at the moment of serving so the fries stay crisp beneath. Born in rural Quebec in the 1950s, poutine is now Canada's defining dish, with variations from classic to foie-gras. The curds must be fresh (same-day, room temperature, squeaky); aged curds and shredded mozzarella are separate, lesser foods.

Ingredients

  • Fries: 4 russets + frying oil + salt (double-fried: 150 °C, then 190 °C)
  • 2 cups fresh cheese curds, room temperature (squeaky!)
  • Gravy: butter + flour + beef/chicken stock + Worcestershire + pepper

Method

  1. Soak cut fries 30+ minutes; dry; first fry at 150 °C until soft and pale.
  2. Rest while you make the gravy: roux, then stock, simmered glossy and peppery.
  3. Second fry at 190 °C until deeply golden and crisp; salt immediately.
  4. Pile fries in warmed bowls; scatter room-temp curds over.
  5. Ladle over steaming-hot gravy — it should soften, not drown, the curds.
  6. Serve within 60 seconds — poutine has no patience.

Chef tips

  • Fresh squeaky curds only — old curds don't melt into strings.
  • Gravy must be steaming — lukewarm gravy leaves cold, rubbery curds.
  • Double-fry the potatoes — single-fried fries collapse under gravy.
  • Assemble to order — even 5 minutes turns it to mush.
