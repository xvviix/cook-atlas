Bitterballen

Netherlands

Bitterballen are Holland's borrel-time icons: small, round, breaded-and-fried croquettes of thick beef ragout (butter-roux, beef stock, shredded slow-cooked beef, parsley, nutmeg, mustard), served scalding with mustard for dipping and beer for drinking. The ragout must chill overnight until sliceable — warm ragout can't be rolled — and the finished balls fry to a shattering crust around a molten center. Bittergarnituur without bitterballen is unthinkable.

Ingredients

  • Ragout: 300 g slow-cooked shredded beef + butter + flour + 500 ml beef stock
  • Parsley + nutmeg + mustard + salt + pepper + gelatin (optional, for slicing)
  • Flour + egg + breadcrumbs for breading; oil at 180 °C; mustard to serve

Method

  1. Slow-cook beef until shreddable; reserve the stock.
  2. Make a thick roux; whisk in stock to a very thick velouté.
  3. Fold in beef, parsley, nutmeg and mustard; chill overnight until firm.
  4. Roll firm ragout into walnut-size balls.
  5. Bread flour-egg-crumb (double-crumb for armor).
  6. Fry at 180 °C until deep gold; rest 2 minutes; serve with mustard.

Chef tips

  • Overnight chill — soft ragout can't be rolled.
  • Thick, thick ragout — thin ragout bursts in the fryer.
  • Double-crumb — the crust is the pressure vessel.
  • Rest before biting — the center is literal lava.
