Goulash

Austria

Wiener Saftgulasch is Austria's dark, intense answer to Hungary's soupier gulyás: beef shank slow-braised with nearly its own weight in onions (the Viennese rule: onions ≥ ¾ of the meat), sweet paprika, caraway, marjoram, garlic, tomato paste and a vinegar-lemon lift — no potatoes or peppers in the pot. The onions dissolve entirely into a thick, mahogany sauce of remarkable depth. Served with bread rolls or Serviettenknödel and beer, preferably eaten in a wood-paneled Beisl.

Ingredients

  • 1.5 kg beef shank, cubed + 1.25 kg onions, sliced
  • 150 g dripping/lard + 4 tbsp sweet paprika + 2 tbsp tomato paste
  • Garlic + caraway + marjoram + juniper + lemon rind + vinegar + flour to bind

Method

  1. Brown the onions deeply golden in dripping — 20–30 minutes, the flavor base.
  2. Stir in paprika off direct heat (it burns bitter in seconds); add paste, garlic, spices.
  3. Deglaze with vinegar and water; add beef; stew covered 2–2.5 hours.
  4. Stir often; add water as needed; the onions should fully dissolve.
  5. Bind lightly with flour slurry; season; serve with rolls or dumplings and beer.

Chef tips

  • Onions nearly equal the meat — skimping gives thin, pale gulasch.
  • Paprika off the heat — scorched paprika turns the whole pot bitter.
  • Low and slow until the onions vanish — rushing leaves crunchy onion and tough beef.
  • Better on day two — like all braises, it deepens overnight.
