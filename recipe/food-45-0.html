Kibbeh

Syria

Syria's national dish in its most iconic fried form (kibbeh maqliya): torpedo-shaped shells of fine bulgur kneaded with lean raw lamb, spices and onion, hollowed by thumb, stuffed with spiced minced meat, pine nuts and pomegranate-bright onion, then deep-fried to a dark, crisp, nutty shell. Aleppo alone claims dozens of kibbeh styles — baked in trays (siniyeh), raw (nayyeh), grilled, in yogurt (labanieh). The shell paste must be smooth and tacky; crumbly paste splits in the oil.

Ingredients

  • Shell: 2 cups fine bulgur + 500 g lean lamb mince + 1 onion + allspice + cumin + salt
  • Filling: 400 g lamb mince + onion + pine nuts + pomegranate molasses + baharat
  • Oil for deep-frying at 175 °C + yogurt + mint to serve

Method

  1. Soak bulgur 20 minutes; squeeze bone-dry through cloth.
  2. Process bulgur with raw meat, onion and spices to a smooth, tacky paste; chill.
  3. Brown filling meat with onion; stir in nuts, molasses and spices; cool fully.
  4. With wet hands, form egg-sized balls; hollow with your thumb into thin cups.
  5. Fill modestly; pinch closed into torpedoes with pointed ends; chill 20 minutes.
  6. Deep-fry at 175 °C until deep mahogany; drain; serve hot with yogurt.

Chef tips

  • Squeeze the bulgur dry — wet bulgur makes shells that burst.
  • Cool filling only — warm filling steams the shell from inside.
  • Thin, even walls — thick spots stay raw-tasting.
  • Chill before frying — cold kibbeh holds its shape in hot oil.
