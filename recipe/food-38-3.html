Fatteh

Egypt

Egyptian fattah is the feast dish of Eid al-Adha: toasted baladi bread moistened with broth, mounded with rice cooked in meat stock, crowned with boiled lamb or beef, and anointed with two sauces — pungent garlic-vinegar (dakka) and tangy tomato. Rich, layered and unapologetically meaty, it is made in vast trays when families gather after the sacrifice. Every layer must be hot at assembly so the bread drinks the broth without turning to mush.

Ingredients

  • 1 kg lamb or beef, boiled + 2 liters of its broth
  • 4 baladi breads/pitas, toasted + 3 cups rice
  • Dakka: 1 head garlic + 60 ml vinegar; tomato sauce: passata + garlic + vinegar

Method

  1. Boil the meat with cardamom, bay and onion until tender; keep the broth hot.
  2. Cook the rice in meat broth until fluffy and separate.
  3. Toast the bread deeply golden; break into a wide serving tray.
  4. Moisten bread with hot broth; mound rice over; arrange meat on top.
  5. Sizzle garlic, deglaze with vinegar for the dakka; spoon over.
  6. Finish with tomato sauce; serve immediately while everything steams.

Chef tips

  • Toast the bread well — pale bread dissolves into paste.
  • Moisten, don't drown — the bread should be juicy with bite.
  • Both sauces are traditional — dakka's punch plus tomato's sweetness.
  • Assemble just before serving — fattah collapses as it stands.
