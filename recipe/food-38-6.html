Ta'meya

Egypt

Egypt's original falafel predates the chickpea kind: ta'meya is made from soaked split fava beans ground with parsley, cilantro, dill, leek and garlic, spiced with cumin and coriander, rolled in sesame and fried into green-centered, crisp patties. Lighter and moister than chickpea falafel, it is the cornerstone of the Egyptian breakfast sandwich — stuffed into baladi bread with tahini, salad and pickles. The sesame crust is the signature crunch.

Ingredients

  • 500 g split dried fava beans, soaked overnight
  • Parsley + cilantro + dill + leek + garlic
  • Cumin + coriander + salt + baking powder; sesame seeds; oil at 175 °C

Method

  1. Soak split favas 12+ hours; drain and dry thoroughly.
  2. Grind with herbs, leek and garlic to a coarse, packable green meal.
  3. Season with cumin, coriander and salt; rest refrigerated 30 minutes.
  4. Fold in baking powder; shape patties; press sesame onto the outside.
  5. Fry at 175 °C until deep gold and crisp; drain.
  6. Stuff into baladi bread with tahini, tomatoes and pickles.

Chef tips

  • Split favas, soaked raw — cooked or canned beans fall apart.
  • Dry the beans well — wet mix spatters and crumbles.
  • Sesame on the outside — it toasts as the patty fries.
  • Fry fresh to order — ta'meya softens within minutes.
