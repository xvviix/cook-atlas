Kabsa

Saudi Arabia

Saudi Arabia's national dish: long-grain basmati cooked in a rich tomato-baharat broth with chicken or lamb, whole spices (black lime, cardamom, cloves, cinnamon), raisins and toasted almonds — the rice stained sunset-orange and fragrant enough to announce dinner from the street. Eaten communally from a vast platter (often with the right hand), kabsa is Friday lunch, wedding feast and everyday comfort at once. The black lime (loomi) is the signature sour depth.

Ingredients

  • 1.5 kg chicken or lamb + 4 cups basmati, soaked
  • Baharat + black lime + cardamom + cloves + cinnamon + bay
  • Tomato + onion + garlic + raisins + almonds + ghee

Method

  1. Brown meat in ghee with onion and whole spices until fragrant.
  2. Add tomato and water; simmer into a rich broth (40+ minutes).
  3. Lift out meat; keep warm (or roast for color).
  4. Add rice to the measured broth; simmer covered 18–20 minutes.
  5. Rest 10 minutes; fluff — grains long, separate, orange.
  6. Mound on a platter; crown with meat, raisins and almonds.

Chef tips

  • Black lime pierced, not whole — it must bleed into the broth.
  • Measure broth to rice — soupy kabsa is a failure.
  • Soak the basmati — soaked grains cook long and unbroken.
  • Roast the meat after — color and texture contrast.
