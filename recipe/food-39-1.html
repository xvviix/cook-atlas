Doro Wat

Ethiopia

Doro wat is Ethiopia's most ceremonial stew — chicken braised in a sauce built from an almost absurd quantity of onions (traditionally dry-cooked down for up to an hour), niter kibbeh (spiced clarified butter), berbere, garlic and ginger, finished with hard-boiled eggs that drink up the sauce. Served at weddings, holidays and honored guests, it simmers low until the sauce is thick enough to mound and brick-red with spice. The onion base is everything: patience here separates transcendent doro wat from merely spicy chicken.

Ingredients

  • 1 kg chicken (bone-in thighs + drumsticks) + lemon for marinating
  • 6 large red onions, minced + 6 tbsp niter kibbeh + 6 tsp berbere
  • Garlic + ginger + stock + 4–6 hard-boiled eggs

Method

  1. Marinate chicken in lemon and salt 30 minutes; rinse and pat dry.
  2. Dry-cook the minced onions, stirring, 30–45 minutes until collapsed and sweet.
  3. Add niter kibbeh, garlic, ginger and berbere; fry into a dark paste.
  4. Add chicken and stock to nearly cover; simmer covered 40–50 minutes.
  5. Add peeled eggs for the last 15 minutes so they absorb the sauce.
  6. Serve on injera with the eggs halved over the top.

Chef tips

  • The onion collapse can't be rushed — high heat scorches instead of sweetening.
  • Berbere varies wildly — taste yours first and adjust quantity.
  • Bone-in chicken enriches the sauce — boneless gives thinner results.
  • Day two is legendary — the sauce deepens overnight.
