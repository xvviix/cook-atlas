Muhammara

Syria

Born in Aleppo: muhammara is the Levant's great red dip — flame-roasted peppers pounded with toasted walnuts, breadcrumbs, pomegranate molasses, garlic, cumin and Aleppo pepper into a thick, sweet-smoky-hot spread glossed with olive oil. Served with bread as part of every mezze, its magic is balance: sweet pepper, sour molasses, heat, crunch. Toasted walnuts (not raw) and real pomegranate molasses separate the sublime from the bland.

Ingredients

  • 4 large red peppers, roasted + 1.5 cups walnuts, toasted
  • ½ cup breadcrumbs + 3 tbsp pomegranate molasses + garlic
  • Cumin + Aleppo pepper + lemon + olive oil + salt

Method

  1. Char peppers until collapsing; steam, peel and squeeze dry.
  2. Toast walnuts until fragrant; cool.
  3. Blitz peppers, walnuts and breadcrumbs to a thick, textured (not smooth) dip.
  4. Work in molasses, garlic, cumin, Aleppo pepper, lemon and oil.
  5. Rest 30 minutes; adjust sweet-sour-heat; serve glossed with oil and walnuts.

Chef tips

  • Squeeze the peppers dry — watery peppers make soup.
  • Toasted walnuts only — raw walnuts taste flat.
  • Textured, not puréed — crunch is part of the pleasure.
  • Pomegranate molasses is irreplaceable — no substitute tastes right.
