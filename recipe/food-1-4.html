Ravioli

Italy

Square fresh-egg pasta parcels with a sealed filling — the classic home version is ricotta and spinach, while four-cheese (ricotta, mozzarella, provolone, Parmesan) is a popular alternative. The keys are pasta rolled thin enough to see your hand through, and an airtight seal so the ravioli don't burst.

Ingredients

  • Pasta: 300 g flour (00 or all-purpose, some semolina optional) + 3 large eggs, pinch of salt, 1 tsp olive oil
  • Ricotta-spinach filling: 400 g ricotta, 200 g cooked squeezed spinach, 60–150 g Parmesan, 1 egg, nutmeg, salt, pepper
  • OR four-cheese filling: ricotta + mozzarella + provolone + Parmesan, 1 egg, parsley
  • Semolina for dusting
  • Sauce to serve: brown butter + sage, or simple tomato, or pesto + cream

Method

  1. Make the dough (~100 g flour per egg): mound flour, add eggs/oil, mix, then knead 8–10 minutes until smooth; wrap and rest 30 minutes at room temperature.
  2. Mix the filling until smooth and well seasoned; chill while rolling the pasta.
  3. Roll the dough into thin even sheets (~1 mm; setting 6–7 on a machine, or by rolling pin).
  4. On one sheet place teaspoon-sized mounds of filling 3–4 cm apart; brush around them with water or beaten egg.
  5. Lay the second sheet over, press out all air around each mound from the filling outward, seal firmly, then cut and crimp edges.
  6. Boil in well-salted water 3–4 minutes until they float plus one minute; lift out gently and toss with the sauce.

Chef tips

  • Expel air pockets — trapped air expands and bursts the seal in boiling water.
  • Dust finished ravioli with semolina and don't stack them; cook within a few hours or freeze.
  • Fresh pasta water should boil vigorously but the ravioli should be handled gently.
