Pasta Carbonara

Italy

Rome's iconic pasta: spaghetti (or rigatoni/bucatini) coated in a silky sauce made only from eggs, Pecorino Romano, rendered guanciale fat and starchy pasta water. Authentic carbonara never contains cream; the creaminess comes from emulsification off the heat.

Ingredients

  • 400 g spaghetti or rigatoni
  • 150–200 g guanciale (cured pork cheek), cut in strips; pancetta as substitute
  • 4 egg yolks + 1 whole egg
  • 80–100 g Pecorino Romano, finely grated
  • Plenty of freshly cracked black pepper
  • Salt for the pasta water

Method

  1. Bring a large pot of lightly salted water to a rolling boil (the guanciale and cheese are already salty).
  2. Crisp the guanciale in a cold pan over medium heat until golden and its fat has rendered, 8–10 minutes; keep warm off the heat.
  3. Whisk yolks, whole egg, Pecorino and a generous amount of pepper into a thick paste; thin with a splash of hot pasta water.
  4. Boil the pasta until just past al dente; reserve at least a cup of pasta water before draining.
  5. Toss the hot pasta through the guanciale and its fat, then — off the heat — pour in the egg mixture, stirring vigorously and loosening with pasta water until glossy and coating, not scrambled.
  6. Plate immediately with extra Pecorino and pepper.

Chef tips

  • Work off the heat when adding eggs — residual heat cooks the sauce; direct heat scrambles it.
  • Guanciale gives the authentic flavor; bacon is a fallback, never ham.
  • Serve at once: carbonara waits for no one.
