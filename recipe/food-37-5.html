Hummus

Palestine

Palestinian hummus bi tahini is the silky, lemony benchmark: chickpeas simmered to collapsing (with baking soda for extra-soft skins) and whipped with generous tahini, lemon, garlic and ice water into a cloud-smooth dip, served warm-ish in a swirled bowl with olive oil, paprika and whole chickpeas. In the West Bank, hummus is breakfast food — scooped with fresh bread, sometimes crowned with fuul or fried meat. Great tahini matters as much as the chickpeas: it should be runny, nutty and fresh.

Ingredients

  • 250 g dried chickpeas + 1 tsp baking soda (or 2 cans + their liquid)
  • 120 ml good tahini + juice of 2 lemons + 1–2 garlic cloves + salt
  • Ice water as needed + olive oil + paprika + parsley to serve

Method

  1. Soak dried chickpeas overnight; simmer with soda 40–60 minutes until falling apart.
  2. Drain (save the liquid); blend chickpeas hot with tahini, lemon, garlic and salt.
  3. Stream in ice water while blending until billowy and pale.
  4. Season boldly — lemon and salt make or break hummus.
  5. Swirl into bowls; pool olive oil in the center; garnish and serve with warm bread.

Chef tips

  • Overcook the chickpeas — 'done' is underdone for hummus.
  • Blend while hot with ice water — the temperature shock makes it fluffy.
  • Tahini quality is half the dish — thick, bitter tahini can't be rescued.
  • Garlic raw and restrained — one clove perfumes; three bully.
