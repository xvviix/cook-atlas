Risotto alla Milanese

Italy

Milan's golden saffron risotto: Carnaroli or Arborio rice toasted in butter, deglazed with white wine, cooked with warm stock ladle by ladle, and finished (mantecatura) with cold butter and Parmigiano. Traditionally served with Osso Buco.

Ingredients

  • 320–400 g Carnaroli or Arborio rice
  • 1–1.5 l warm chicken or beef stock
  • 1 generous pinch saffron threads (~0.5 g)
  • 1 small onion, finely chopped
  • 50–80 g butter (half for cooking, half cold for finishing)
  • 25 g beef bone marrow (optional, traditional)
  • 120 ml dry white wine
  • 50 g Parmigiano-Reggiano, grated
  • Salt, pepper

Method

  1. Steep the saffron in a small cup of hot stock or water 15–30 minutes for full color and aroma.
  2. Sweat the onion (and marrow, if using) in half the butter over low heat until soft and translucent, 5–10 minutes.
  3. Add the rice (tostatura) and stir 1–2 minutes until grains are glistening and translucent at the edges.
  4. Pour in the wine with the saffron liquid; stir until absorbed.
  5. Add warm stock one ladle at a time, stirring often and letting each addition absorb before adding more, about 18 minutes, until al dente and flowing (all'onda).
  6. Off the heat, beat in the cold butter and Parmigiano (mantecatura); rest 1–2 minutes and serve immediately on warm plates.

Chef tips

  • Keep the stock at a bare simmer throughout — cold stock shocks the rice.
  • Stir regularly but not violently; the goal is creamy, loose, and glossy, never gluey.
  • Saffron quality matters more than quantity; avoid powdered 'coloring' substitutes.
