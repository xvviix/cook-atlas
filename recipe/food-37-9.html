Warak Enab

Palestine

Palestinian warak dawali (grape leaves) are the meat-and-rice kind: vine leaves wrapped tight around short-grain rice, minced lamb, allspice and olive oil, packed seam-down over lamb chops or potatoes, and braised in lemony broth until tender and glistening. Rolled small and neat — a point of pride — they're cooked low until the rice swells silkily inside. Served warm with more lemon, they're the labor-of-love centerpiece of family gatherings.

Ingredients

  • 1 jar grape leaves + 2 cups short-grain rice, rinsed
  • 500 g minced lamb + allspice + cinnamon + olive oil + salt + pepper
  • Lamb chops/potatoes for lining + 3 lemons + broth + tomato (optional)

Method

  1. Rinse and blanch the leaves; trim tough stems.
  2. Mix rice with lamb, spices and olive oil — the filling goes in raw.
  3. Roll small and tight: stem-end up, fold sides, roll to the tip.
  4. Line the pot with chops or potatoes; pack rolls seam-down in snug layers.
  5. Weigh down with a plate; add lemony broth to cover; simmer 45–60 minutes.
  6. Rest 15 minutes; invert or lift out; serve with lemon.

Chef tips

  • Small and tight is the pride — fat, loose rolls burst.
  • Pack snugly — gaps let rolls tumble and unwrap.
  • The plate weight is essential — it holds everything through the simmer.
  • Short-grain rice only — long grain cooks unevenly inside the leaf.
