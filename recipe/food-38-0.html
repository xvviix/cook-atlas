Koshari

Egypt

Egypt's national dish is joyful carb-on-carb architecture: brown lentils and rice cooked together, small pasta, chickpeas, a garlicky cumin-tomato salsa, sharp vinegar-garlic dakka, and a blizzard of shatteringly crisp fried onions — with the reserved onion oil flavoring every layer. Born of Cairo's street carts and now served everywhere from stalls to dedicated koshari institutions, it is vegan, cheap, filling and completely addictive. The crispy onions are non-negotiable: flour-dusted, fried dark gold, and piled high.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup brown lentils + 1 cup short-grain rice + 1 cup vermicelli + 2 cups small pasta
  • 3 onions, flour-dusted and fried crisp (save the oil!) + 1 can chickpeas
  • Tomato salsa: garlic + tomato + cumin + coriander + vinegar; dakka: garlic + vinegar + cumin + chile

Method

  1. Fry the floured onions dark gold and crisp; drain; reserve every drop of the oil.
  2. Parboil lentils; add rice and vermicelli toasted in onion oil; steam until tender.
  3. Boil pasta separately; toss with onion oil so it never sticks.
  4. Simmer the tomato salsa until thick and garlicky; mix the dakka.
  5. Warm the chickpeas in salsa; layer rice-lentils, pasta, chickpeas.
  6. Ladle salsa over; crown with crispy onions; serve dakka and hot sauce alongside.

Chef tips

  • The onion oil is the secret — it flavors rice, pasta and sauce.
  • Fry onions dark gold, not pale — pale onions are chewy, not crisp.
  • Keep components separate until serving — mixed koshari steams itself soggy.
  • Dakka on the side — its vinegar punch is personal.
