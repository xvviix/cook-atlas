Sushi

Japan

Listed here as nigiri, sushi's purest form: seasoned rice hand-pressed into bite-sized ovals and draped with sashimi-grade fish. The rice (shari) matters as much as the fish — served at body temperature, loosely packed so it melts apart.

Ingredients

  • 300 g short-grain sushi rice + 375 ml water
  • Seasoning: 2 tbsp rice vinegar + 1 tbsp sugar + ½ tsp salt
  • 300 g sashimi-grade salmon/tuna, sliced 4×8 cm
  • Wasabi; soy sauce; pickled ginger
  • Hand water: cold water + splash of vinegar

Method

  1. Rinse the rice until the water runs clear; cook and rest 10 minutes.
  2. Fold in the warmed seasoning with cutting strokes while fanning to cool — glossy, room-temperature grains.
  3. Wet your hands; take ~2 tbsp rice and shape a loose oval — press, never squeeze.
  4. Dab wasabi on the fish, drape over the rice, and press gently with two fingers to bond.
  5. Serve immediately with soy and ginger; nigiri waits for no one.

Chef tips

  • Rice at body temperature + cold fish = the contrast sushi is built on.
  • Over-packed rice is the classic home failure — airy beats dense.
  • A razor-sharp knife and single-stroke fish cuts keep edges clean.
