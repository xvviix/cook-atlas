Jamón Ibérico

Spain

A cured-ham product, not a cooked recipe: hind legs of Ibérico pigs, fattened on acorns during the montanera, then salted and cured 1–4 years. Bellota (acorn-fed, free-range) is the summit; cebo is grain-fed. This card covers production plus buying, carving and serving.

Ingredients

  • Whole bone-in leg (or hand-carved slices)
  • Jamonero stand + long flexible slicing knife
  • To serve: room temperature, plain bread, nothing else needed

Method

  1. Rearing: Ibérico pigs roam the dehesa oak pasture, gorging on acorns (bellota) in autumn — this creates the marbled, nutty fat.
  2. Salazón: legs are buried in sea salt days to weeks by weight (dehydration, not seasoning).
  3. Lavado + post-salado: washed, shaped, then rested cold 1–3 months while salt equalizes.
  4. Curado: hung in drying lofts and cellars 1–4 years (bellota longest), where enzymes build aroma and fat melts into the muscle.
  5. At home: temper slices 30 minutes at room temperature; carve paper-thin across the grain; drape, never stack.

Chef tips

  • Look for the black-tag 'pata negra' 100% ibérico de bellota for the peak; red/green/white tags step down.
  • The fat is the flavor — translucent, sweet, and meant to melt on the tongue.
  • Never serve fridge-cold; cold mutes every aroma the years built.
