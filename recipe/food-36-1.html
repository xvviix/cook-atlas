Sachertorte

Austria

Invented in 1832 by 16-year-old apprentice Franz Sacher for Prince Metternich, the Sachertorte is Vienna's most storied cake: dense chocolate sponge layered and enrobed with apricot jam, sealed under a mirror of dark chocolate glaze, and served with unsweetened whipped cream. The Hotel Sacher and Demel fought a famous seven-year legal battle over the 'Original' title — Sacher won. The jam layer is structural as well as flavorful: it smooths the crumb so the glaze pours glass-perfect.

Ingredients

  • Cake: 115 g dark chocolate + 115 g butter + 6 eggs, separated + 120 g flour + sugar + vanilla
  • Filling/coating: 300–500 g strained apricot jam (+ rum optional)
  • Glaze: 200 g dark chocolate + butter/cream; whipped cream to serve

Method

  1. Melt chocolate with butter; whisk in yolks, sugar and vanilla.
  2. Whip whites to soft peaks; fold in alternately with flour.
  3. Bake in a lined 22 cm tin at 170 °C for 50–60 minutes; cool completely.
  4. Split horizontally; spread jam between layers and over the entire cake; let dry 1 hour.
  5. Pour warm chocolate glaze over in one smooth motion; do not touch up.
  6. Rest overnight for clean slicing; serve with unsweetened schlagobers.

Chef tips

  • Let the jam dry to the touch — wet jam bleeds through the glaze.
  • Pour glaze once, confidently — spatula touch-ups leave streaks.
  • Unsweetened cream is mandatory — it balances the intense chocolate.
  • Day-two slices cut cleanest — the glaze fully sets overnight.
