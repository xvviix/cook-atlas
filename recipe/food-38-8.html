Basbousa

Egypt

Basbousa (hareesa/revani across the region) is the beloved semolina cake: coarse semolina enriched with butter or ghee, yogurt and coconut, baked golden with an almond pressed into each diamond, then flooded with lemon-rose sharbat syrup while screaming hot. The hot-cake/cool-syrup collision is what makes it molten-moist rather than merely sweet. Served at room temperature in every Egyptian patisserie, it keeps for days — if it survives the afternoon.

Ingredients

  • Cake: 2 cups coarse semolina + 1 cup sugar + 1 cup yogurt + 100 g melted ghee
  • 1 cup desiccated coconut + 2 tsp baking powder + almonds for topping
  • Sharbat: 2 cups sugar + 1.5 cups water + lemon + rose water

Method

  1. Mix semolina, sugar, coconut and baking powder; stir in yogurt and ghee to a thick batter.
  2. Rest 15–30 minutes so the semolina hydrates.
  3. Spread in a buttered pan; score diamonds; press an almond into each.
  4. Bake at 180 °C for 30–40 minutes until deep gold edge to edge.
  5. Flood with cool syrup the instant it leaves the oven.
  6. Cool fully so the syrup absorbs; re-cut and serve at room temperature.

Chef tips

  • Hot cake + cool syrup — the temperature shock drives absorption.
  • Coarse semolina gives the classic grainy bite — fine semolina turns cakey.
  • Score before baking — cutting baked basbousa shatters it.
  • Don't skimp the syrup — dry basbousa is just sweet cornbread.
