Harissa

Tunisia

Tunisia's national condiment — UNESCO-recognized since 2022: sun-dried Baklouti peppers rehydrated and pounded with garlic, coriander, caraway and salt into a thick, brick-red, smoky-sweet paste, sealed under olive oil. Hotter and more aromatic than generic chili paste, it seasons couscous, lablabi, ojja and grilled fish — and stirred with olive oil becomes the dip for bread that opens every Tunisian meal. Homemade keeps months under oil in the fridge.

Ingredients

  • 200 g dried Baklouti (or guajillo/New Mexico) chiles
  • 1 head garlic + 2 tbsp coriander + 1 tbsp caraway + salt
  • Olive oil to cover (lots)

Method

  1. Stem and seed the chiles; soak in hot water 30–60 minutes until supple.
  2. Drain and squeeze very dry — water is the only spoilage risk.
  3. Pound or blend with garlic, spices and salt to a thick paste.
  4. Work in olive oil until glossy; pack into sterilized jars.
  5. Cover with a 1 cm oil cap; refrigerate; keep oil-topped after each use.

Chef tips

  • Squeeze the chiles dry — trapped water spoils the paste.
  • Caraway is the Tunisian signature — cumin alone tastes generic.
  • Maintain the oil cap — exposed paste molds.
  • Age a week before judging — the flavors marry and deepen.
