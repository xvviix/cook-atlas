Dim Sum

China

Canton's brunch ritual of small steamed plates — covered here through its two flagships: har gow (crystal shrimp dumplings in wheat-starch skin) and siu mai (open pork-shrimp cups). Bamboo steamers, tea, and eating the moment each basket lands.

Ingredients

  • Har gow skins: wheat starch + tapioca + boiling water + oil + salt
  • Har gow filling: shrimp + bamboo shoots + ginger + sesame oil
  • Siu mai: wonton skins + pork + shrimp + soy + oyster sauce + sesame + sugar
  • Bamboo steamer + parchment/cabbage liners
  • Chili oil + soy + vinegar dips; tea

Method

  1. Mince fillings to a sticky paste; chill — cold filling shapes cleaner.
  2. Knead the wheat-starch dough smooth; roll thin rounds, keeping all dough covered (it dries in minutes).
  3. Har gow: half-moon with 7–10 pleats; siu mai: ruffled open cup, tapped flat to stand.
  4. Steam over simmering water — har gow 6 minutes, siu mai ~12 — lids on, no peeking.
  5. Serve in the baskets immediately with dips and tea.

Chef tips

  • Wheat-starch dough waits for no one — cover every scrap you're not rolling.
  • Space the dumplings; crowding glues them together.
  • Fresh-from-steamer only — dim sum stiffens as it stands.
