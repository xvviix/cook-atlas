Ful Medames

Egypt

Ful medames — slow-stewed fava beans — is Egypt's ancient breakfast, eaten since Pharaonic times and still ladled from giant brass pots (qidra) every morning across the country. Dried favas simmer for hours with lentils until creamy, then get mashed coarsely and dressed with olive oil, lemon, cumin and garlic, with tahini, eggs, tomatoes and pickles as classic accompaniments. Scooped with baladi bread, it is protein-rich fuel that keeps Cairo moving until lunch.

Ingredients

  • 500 g dried fava beans, soaked overnight + 2 tbsp red lentils + 1 tomato
  • Dressing: olive oil + lemon + cumin + garlic + salt
  • To serve: tahini + boiled eggs + tomatoes + onion + pickles + baladi bread

Method

  1. Soak favas 8–12 hours; rinse and pick over.
  2. Simmer with lentils, tomato and garlic 3–4 hours (or pressure-cook 40 minutes) until creamy.
  3. Mash coarsely — ful should be creamy with texture, not puréed.
  4. Dress generously with olive oil, lemon, cumin and salt.
  5. Serve in bowls with tahini swirled in and all accompaniments alongside.

Chef tips

  • Long, slow cooking makes creamy ful — rushing leaves tough skins.
  • Lentils dissolve into body — they thicken without flour.
  • Lemon and cumin at serving, not during cooking — their brightness fades with heat.
  • Leftovers fry brilliantly into next-day ful cakes.
