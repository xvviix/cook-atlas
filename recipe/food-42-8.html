Ash Reshteh

Iran

Ash reshteh is Iran's great noodle soup — thick with reshteh noodles, three beans, lentils and a garden of herbs and spinach, crowned with golden fried onions, mint sizzled in oil (nana dagh) and swirls of kashk (fermented whey). Made for Sizdah Bedar picnics and shared with neighbors (the noodles symbolize life's threads), it must be thick enough to mound — thin ash is just soup. The toppings aren't garnish; they're half the dish.

Ingredients

  • Reshteh noodles + chickpeas + kidney beans + lentils, cooked
  • Herbs: parsley + cilantro + spinach + scallions, chopped fine
  • Kashk + fried onions + mint sizzled in oil + turmeric + garlic

Method

  1. Simmer beans and lentils with turmeric until tender.
  2. Add half the herbs; simmer 20 minutes.
  3. Add noodles; cook until soft and the soup thickens substantially.
  4. Season boldly; the ash should mound on a spoon.
  5. Bowl up; crown with kashk, fried onions and sizzling mint oil.

Chef tips

  • Thick enough to mound — thin ash misses the point.
  • Reshteh noodles are traditional — they release starch that thickens.
  • Toppings in quantity — kashk, onions and mint make it.
  • Cook the mint in oil until fragrant — raw mint tastes flat.
