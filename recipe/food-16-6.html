Cao lầu

Vietnam

Hội An's unexportable noodle bowl: thick chewy noodles made only with water from the Bá Lễ well and local lye ash, tossed with five-spice char-siu pork, a ladle of rich broth, greens, herbs and crackling croutons. Outside Hội An, it's homage — udon subs in.

Ingredients

  • 400 g cao lầu noodles (or thick udon as substitute)
  • 500 g pork belly/loin: five-spice + soy + fish sauce + oyster + sugar, braised then seared
  • Rich pork broth; lettuce + bean sprouts + mint + basil
  • Crispy croutons (fried noodle squares/pork crackling) + peanuts + lime + chile

Method

  1. Braise the marinated pork 45–60 minutes; sear slices to caramelized edges; reserve broth.
  2. Blanch noodles and sprouts.
  3. Bowl: greens first, then noodles, pork slices and herbs.
  4. Ladle just enough hot broth to moisten — cao lầu is dressed, not soupy.
  5. Top with crackling, peanuts, lime and chile; toss at the table.

Chef tips

  • Little broth is correct — drowning it misses the dish entirely.
  • The chew of real cao lầu can't be replicated; embrace the homage.
  • Sear the braised pork — the caramel crust against soft noodles is the contrast.
