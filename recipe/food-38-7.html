Fesikh

Egypt

Fesikh is Egypt's most notorious delicacy: grey mullet dry-salted and fermented in barrels for up to three weeks until powerfully pungent, then eaten — uncooked — at Sham el-Nessim, the spring festival dating to Pharaonic times. The fish is soaked to tame the salt, flaked and dressed with olive oil, lemon and cumin, scooped with bread, scallions and pickles. Homemade fesikh carries real food-safety risk (botulism warnings are issued yearly); reputable fishmongers (fasakhani) are the safe source.

Ingredients

  • 1 whole salted fesikh from a trusted fasakhani
  • Olive oil + lemon + cumin + coriander
  • To serve: baladi bread + scallions + pickles + tahini

Method

  1. Buy from a reputable fasakhani — never dubious roadside fish.
  2. Soak 1–2 hours, changing water, to draw out excess salt.
  3. Fillet and flake, discarding skin and all bones meticulously.
  4. Dress with olive oil, lemon, cumin and coriander; chill 30 minutes.
  5. Serve with bread, scallions and pickles; eat the same day.

Chef tips

  • Source is everything — bad fesikh is genuinely dangerous.
  • Soak to taste — oversalted fesikh is inedible.
  • Bone-pick carefully — fine mullet bones hide in the flakes.
  • Eat fresh the same day — dressed fesikh doesn't keep.
