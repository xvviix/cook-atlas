Lasagna

Italy

Listed here in its birthplace form, Lasagne alla Bolognese from Emilia-Romagna: thin fresh egg pasta (traditionally green spinach pasta), layered with slow-cooked ragù Bolognese, delicate besciamella (béchamel) and Parmigiano-Reggiano — lighter and more layered than the ricotta-heavy versions common abroad.

Ingredients

  • Fresh egg lasagne sheets (about 250 g flour + 2–3 eggs, or store-bought fresh sheets); spinach in the dough for the green Bolognese tradition
  • Ragù: 300 g each minced beef and pork, 100 g pancetta, soffritto (onion, carrot, celery), 300 g tomato passata, 120 ml milk, 120 ml white wine
  • Besciamella: 70 g butter, 70 g flour, 750 ml milk, nutmeg, salt
  • 100–150 g Parmigiano-Reggiano, grated
  • Butter dots for the top

Method

  1. Make the ragù: sweat the soffritto in butter/oil, brown the meats, deglaze with wine, add tomato and simmer gently at least 2 hours, stirring in the milk near the end; season.
  2. Make the besciamella: melt butter, whisk in flour 1–2 minutes, then whisk in hot milk until smooth; simmer until it coats a spoon; season with salt and nutmeg.
  3. Roll fresh pasta very thin (almost translucent) and blanch sheets briefly if very thick; lay out on clean towels.
  4. Assemble in a buttered ~30×20 cm dish: thin ragù, pasta, besciamella, Parmigiano — repeat to 5 layers; finish with ragù, Parmigiano and butter dots.
  5. Bake at 170 °C (338 °F) about 40 minutes until golden and bubbling; rest 10–15 minutes before cutting so layers set.

Chef tips

  • Real Bolognese lasagna uses no ricotta and no thick noodles — thinness is the point.
  • The corner pieces with crisped edges are traditionally the most prized.
  • Use a glass/ceramic dish, not reactive aluminum, because of the tomato acidity.
