Fesenjan

Iran

Fesenjan (fesenjoon) is Persia's ancient sweet-sour masterpiece: chicken or duck braised in a thick, mahogany sauce of finely ground toasted walnuts and pomegranate molasses, balanced between tart, sweet and deeply nutty. A Yalda-night and wedding classic with roots in Gilan, it simmers low for over an hour while the walnuts release their oil and the sauce turns glossy. The sweet-sour balance is personal — Gilanis favor sour, others sweeter.

Ingredients

  • 1 kg chicken thighs/drumsticks + 300 g walnuts, toasted and finely ground
  • 250 ml pomegranate molasses + 2–3 tbsp sugar + turmeric + cinnamon
  • Onion + stock + saffron + salt + pepper + pomegranate seeds to garnish

Method

  1. Toast walnuts; grind to a fine, almost-buttery meal.
  2. Simmer walnuts in stock 20 minutes, stirring, until thickened.
  3. Brown the chicken with onion and turmeric; add to the walnut pot.
  4. Stir in pomegranate molasses and sugar; simmer 60–75 minutes.
  5. Balance: more molasses for sour, more sugar for sweet.
  6. Serve over chelo, scattered with pomegranate seeds.

Chef tips

  • Grind walnuts fine — coarse meal makes gritty sauce.
  • Low and slow — the walnut oil must release for gloss.
  • Balance at the end — pomegranate molasses varies wildly.
  • Better the next day — like all Persian stews, it deepens.
