Musakhan

Palestine

Palestine's national dish: sumac-roasted chicken on taboon flatbread buried under sweet onions caramelized in extraordinary amounts of olive oil, showered with more sumac and toasted pine nuts. Born of the olive harvest — its three pillars are olive oil, sumac and village bread — musakhan tastes of the Palestinian hillsides: fruity, tangy, deeply savory. The bread underneath drinks the onion oil and becomes, many argue, the best part.

Ingredients

  • 1 whole chicken, jointed (or 6 leg quarters) + 120 ml olive oil
  • 5–6 large onions, thickly sliced + 4–5 tbsp sumac + 2 tsp baharat + lemon
  • Taboon/markouk bread + pine nuts + parsley; salt + pepper + cumin

Method

  1. Rub chicken with oil, sumac, baharat, lemon, salt and pepper; roast at 200 °C for 35–45 minutes.
  2. Meanwhile, soften the onions low and slow in generous olive oil until sweet and golden.
  3. Stir sumac, baharat and cumin through the onions off the heat.
  4. Toast pine nuts in olive oil until golden; set aside.
  5. Layer bread on a platter; pile on sumac onions; crown with chicken.
  6. Scatter pine nuts and parsley; drizzle with pan juices; serve immediately.

Chef tips

  • Onions sliced thick, not diced — they should melt, not dissolve.
  • Be lavish with olive oil — stinginess is the only real failure mode.
  • Good sumac is brick-red and lemony — brown, dusty sumac is dead.
  • Warm the bread before assembling so it drinks the juices.
