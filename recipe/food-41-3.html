Shakshouka

Tunisia

Before it conquered brunch menus worldwide, shakshouka was Tunisian ojja's egg-crowned sibling: peppers, tomatoes, onions and garlic simmered with cumin, paprika and harissa into a thick, sweet-spicy sauce, with eggs poached directly in wells until the whites set and yolks stay molten. Served in the pan with crusty bread for swiping, it is dinner, breakfast or whatever-you-need. The sauce must be thick before the eggs go in — watery sauce means watery eggs.

Ingredients

  • 2 bell peppers + 800 g tomatoes + 1 onion + 4 garlic cloves
  • 1 tbsp cumin + paprika + 1–2 tsp harissa + olive oil
  • 6 eggs + feta/parsley optional + crusty bread

Method

  1. Soften onions and peppers in olive oil 10–12 minutes.
  2. Add garlic, cumin, paprika and harissa; bloom 1 minute.
  3. Add tomatoes; simmer 15–20 minutes until thick enough to hold a well.
  4. Make wells; crack in eggs; cover and poach 5–8 minutes.
  5. Whites set, yolks runny; scatter feta/parsley; serve in the pan with bread.

Chef tips

  • Thicken the sauce first — thin sauce poaches eggs badly.
  • Lid on for the eggs — steam sets the tops without overcooking yolks.
  • Runny yolks are the sauce's second sauce — don't overcook.
  • Bread is mandatory — a fork alone wastes the best part.
