Gnocchi

Italy

Small potato dumplings ('knuckles') — light and pillowy when made with dry-baked (not boiled) potatoes, riced while hot, and mixed with a minimum of flour. Overworking or too much flour is what makes gnocchi dense and gummy.

Ingredients

  • 1 kg russet/baking potatoes (3–4)
  • 1 large egg (or 2 yolks), lightly beaten
  • 150–200 g all-purpose flour, plus more for dusting
  • 1 tsp fine sea salt
  • To serve: brown butter + sage, pesto, or tomato sauce + Parmesan

Method

  1. Bake the potatoes at 200 °C (400 °F) ~1 hour until completely tender (baking keeps them dry; boiling waterlogs them).
  2. While still hot, peel and rice/mash until perfectly smooth with no lumps.
  3. Season, add the egg, then sprinkle over the flour and mix gently with a fork, then briefly by hand, into a soft, slightly tacky (never sticky) dough — stop as soon as it holds together.
  4. Divide into portions; roll each into ~2 cm ropes on a floured surface and cut into 2 cm pillows; optionally roll over a fork or gnocchi board for ridges that catch sauce.
  5. Boil in well-salted water in batches; they are done ~1 minute after floating to the surface. Lift out with a slotted spoon.
  6. Toss immediately with the sauce (and optionally pan-sear in butter for crisp edges); serve at once.

Chef tips

  • Work the potatoes hot and the dough little — heat + minimal flour = lightness.
  • If the dough needs lots of extra flour, the potatoes were too wet; next time bake longer.
  • Freeze uncooked gnocchi on a floured tray, then bag; boil from frozen.
