Gouda

Netherlands

Gouda — Holland's 800-year-old washed-curd cheese, named for the market city: pasteurized cow's milk set with rennet, the curd cut, stirred and washed with warm water (the washing gives Gouda its sweet, mild, elastic character), pressed into wheels, brined, waxed or rind-aged from jong (4 weeks, mild and creamy) to oud/overjarig (12+ months, crystalline, butterscotch-sharp). Home cheesemaking is possible with cultures, rennet, a press and patience — aging decides everything.

Ingredients

  • 10 liters whole milk + mesophilic culture + rennet + salt brine
  • Cheesecloth + mold + press + wax for aging
  • Aging space at 12–14 °C

Method

  1. Warm milk to 30 °C; stir in culture; ripen 30 minutes.
  2. Add rennet; set 45–60 minutes to a clean break.
  3. Cut curds; stir; wash with warm water (the Gouda step).
  4. Drain; pack into molds; press 6–12 hours, increasing weight.
  5. Brine 12–24 hours; air-dry; wax.
  6. Age 4 weeks (jong) to 12+ months (oud), turning regularly.

Chef tips

  • Wash the curd — it's what makes Gouda Gouda.
  • Press firmly — loose pressing traps whey and sours.
  • Age cool and humid — warm aging cracks and bitters.
  • Patience — young is mild, old is glory.
