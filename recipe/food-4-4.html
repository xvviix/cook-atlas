Pastéis de Nata

Portugal

Portugal's world-famous custard tarts: shatteringly flaky pastry cups filled with wobbly lemon-cinnamon custard, baked at extreme heat until leopard-spotted. (Pastel de Belém is the trademarked original from Belém; pastéis de nata is the generic name used everywhere else.)

Ingredients

  • 300 g puff pastry
  • 1¼ cups whole milk + ⅓ cup flour + pinch of salt
  • 1⅓ cups sugar + ⅓ cup water + cinnamon stick + lemon peel
  • 6 egg yolks + 1 tsp vanilla
  • Cinnamon + powdered sugar to serve

Method

  1. Make the syrup: boil sugar, water, cinnamon and lemon 3 minutes; discard aromatics.
  2. Cook milk with flour and salt until thick; off the heat whisk in yolks and vanilla, then the warm syrup in a thin stream; strain.
  3. Roll pastry into a tight log, cut 3 cm pieces, and press thin into buttered molds; chill.
  4. Heat the oven to its maximum (250–300 °C) with a tray inside; fill shells ¾ full.
  5. Bake 12–20 minutes until golden with signature dark caramel spots; cool briefly and serve warm dusted with cinnamon.

Chef tips

  • Puff pastry from the store works well — the oven heat matters more than homemade lamination.
  • Fill only ¾ full: the custard puffs dramatically and overflows if overfilled.
  • Top-rack position + preheated tray = crisp bottoms, spotted tops.
