Shopska Salad

Bulgaria

Bulgaria's most famous salad is a study in freshness: ripe tomatoes, cucumber, sweet peppers and red onion dressed with nothing but good olive oil and salt, then buried under a thick snowfall of finely grated sirene cheese. The red-white-green layers deliberately echo the Bulgarian flag, and the dish was popularized in its modern form by state tourism restaurants in the 1950s. It arrives at nearly every Bulgarian table as the opening act to grilled meats — cool, crisp, salty and bright.

Ingredients

  • 3 large ripe tomatoes, cubed + 1 cucumber, half-moon slices
  • 1 sweet green or red pepper, cubed + ½ red onion, finely chopped + olives (optional)
  • 150–200 g sirene, finely grated + 60 ml extra-virgin olive oil + salt

Method

  1. Cube the tomatoes and pepper; slice the cucumber into half-moons for the classic look.
  2. Chop the onion finely (soak briefly in cold water if harsh) and combine all vegetables.
  3. Dress with olive oil and salt only — vinegar is optional and non-traditional in most versions.
  4. Mound onto a platter and blanket completely with grated sirene; do not toss after cheesing.
  5. Serve immediately while the vegetables are crisp and cold.

Chef tips

  • Grate the sirene fine — crumbled chunks don't give the signature snowcap.
  • Summer tomatoes make or break it — out of season, use the ripest cherry tomatoes you can find.
  • Cheese goes on last, unmixed — tossing after cheesing turns it cloudy.
  • Pair with kebapche or kyufte and cold beer for the full Bulgarian grill experience.
