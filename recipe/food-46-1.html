Chakhchoukha

Algeria

Chakhchoukha (not the egg dish chakchouka!) is eastern Algeria's torn-bread feast: thin semolina flatbread (rougag) torn into bite-size pieces and steamed through a rich lamb, chickpea, tomato and pepper stew until the bread drinks the sauce but keeps its chew. Festive, communal and deeply comforting — Biskra's version is the most famous. Slightly stale or griddled-day-old rougag holds up best; fresh soft bread turns to mush.

Ingredients

  • Rougag: 500 g fine semolina + water + salt (or store flatbread)
  • 700 g lamb + onion + tomato + chickpeas + peppers + carrots
  • Ras el hanout + paprika + cumin + harissa + coriander

Method

  1. Knead semolina dough; rest; roll paper-thin and griddle into rougag.
  2. Brown lamb with onion and spices; add tomato and water; simmer 45 minutes.
  3. Add chickpeas and vegetables; simmer until tender.
  4. Tear rougag into pieces; fold through the stew off the heat.
  5. Rest covered 10 minutes so bread drinks sauce.
  6. Serve mounded with extra sauce and harissa.

Chef tips

  • Day-old rougag drinks best — fresh bread goes mushy.
  • Tear bite-size — big sheets tangle, crumbs vanish.
  • Rest off heat — the marriage happens in the rest.
  • Harissa on the side — heat is personal.
