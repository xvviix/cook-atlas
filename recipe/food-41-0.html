Couscous

Tunisia

Tunisian couscous is Friday's sacred lunch: fine semolina pearls steamed three times over a bubbling lamb-and-vegetable broth (couscoussier), each steaming separated by rolling with oil and water, until every grain is distinct, fluffy and fragrant — then mounded, crowned with meat, chickpeas and carrots, and moistened with broth plus harissa on the side. The triple steam is non-negotiable tradition; instant 'just-add-water' couscous is a different, lesser grain. Tunisia favors a soupier, spicier, tomato-rich broth than its neighbors.

Ingredients

  • 500 g fine couscous + olive oil
  • 1 kg lamb + onions + tomato paste + harissa + chickpeas
  • Carrots + zucchini + potatoes + turnips + cumin + coriander + caraway

Method

  1. Brown lamb with onions, tomato paste and harissa; add water and chickpeas; simmer.
  2. Roll dry couscous with oil and salted water; rest 10 minutes.
  3. Steam 20 minutes (1st); tip out, roll with water and oil, break lumps.
  4. Steam 20 minutes (2nd); repeat rolling; add vegetables to the broth.
  5. Steam 15–20 minutes (3rd); butter the grains until separate and glistening.
  6. Mound on a platter; arrange meat and vegetables; moisten with broth.

Chef tips

  • Three steams, three rollings — shortcuts make porridge, not couscous.
  • Break every lump while rolling — lumps steam into dumplings.
  • Broth on the side — couscous keeps drinking at the table.
  • Harissa is the seasoning — stir it into your own portion.
