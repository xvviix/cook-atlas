Tiramisù

Italy

Italy’s most famous spoon dessert ('pick me up'): layers of espresso-soaked savoiardi ladyfingers and a cloud of mascarpone-egg cream, dusted with cocoa. No-bake, served chilled; the classic uses raw yolks whipped with sugar (use pasteurized eggs), with optional Marsala, rum or coffee liqueur.

Ingredients

  • 500 g mascarpone, cold
  • 5 egg yolks (pasteurized) + 100–140 g sugar
  • 250–400 ml cold heavy cream (many modern recipes; optional but stabilizes)
  • 250–300 g crisp savoiardi ladyfingers (~24–28)
  • 300 ml strong espresso, cooled
  • 2–4 tbsp Marsala, dark rum or coffee liqueur (optional)
  • 1 tsp vanilla, pinch of salt
  • Unsweetened cocoa powder for dusting

Method

  1. Brew the espresso, stir in liqueur if using, and cool completely.
  2. Whisk yolks with sugar 5+ minutes until pale, thick ribbons (warm over a bain-marie to 70 °C if food-safety is a concern).
  3. Loosen the mascarpone with the vanilla and salt, fold into the yolks, then fold in softly whipped cream until smooth and holding soft peaks.
  4. Quickly dip each savoiardo (1 second per side — they keep absorbing) and line the dish; spread over half the cream.
  5. Repeat a second layer of dipped ladyfingers + remaining cream; smooth the top.
  6. Cover and chill at least 4 hours, ideally overnight; dust generously with cocoa just before serving.

Chef tips

  • Dip, don't soak: soggy ladyfingers collapse the layers.
  • Use crisp savoiardi, not soft sponge fingers, and keep everything cold while assembling.
  • Keeps 2–3 days refrigerated; the flavor is best on day two.
