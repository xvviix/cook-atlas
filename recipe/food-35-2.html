Kavarma

Bulgaria

Kavarma is Bulgaria's answer to slow comfort: chunks of pork (or chicken) braised with leeks, sweet peppers, mushrooms, tomatoes and onions in red wine, traditionally finished in individual clay pots (gyuvech) so each serving arrives bubbling. Paprika and savory (chubritsa) give it its warm Balkan depth, and the long braise melts the vegetables into a rich, spoonable sauce. It is winter food at its best — hearty, rustic and made for mopping up with bread.

Ingredients

  • 800 g pork shoulder, cubed + 5 leeks, sliced + 3 red + 3 green peppers
  • 250 g mushrooms + 400 g tomatoes + 2 onions + 3 garlic cloves
  • 120 ml red wine + 250 ml stock + 2 tbsp tomato paste + 1 tbsp paprika + chubritsa/savory + oil + butter

Method

  1. Sear the pork in batches until browned; set aside (crowding steams instead of searing).
  2. Soften leeks, onions and peppers; add garlic, paprika and tomato paste and fry 2 minutes.
  3. Deglaze with red wine; add stock, tomatoes and mushrooms.
  4. Return the pork; braise covered on low 60–90 minutes until spoon-tender.
  5. Transfer to clay pots if you have them; bake 20 minutes at 180 °C to finish.
  6. Rest 10 minutes; shower with parsley and serve with crusty bread.

Chef tips

  • Leeks are the soul of kavarma — don't substitute them away.
  • Brown the meat properly — the fond is the foundation of the sauce.
  • Clay pots hold heat beautifully — preheat them so the stew keeps bubbling at the table.
  • Even better the next day — the flavors meld overnight.
