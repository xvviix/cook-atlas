Warak Enab

Syria

Syrian yabraq (warak enab) are the meat-and-rice grape leaves: vine leaves rolled tight around short-grain rice, minced lamb, allspice and baharat, packed over lamb chops or a garlic bed and braised in lemony broth with dried mint until tender and glistening. Aleppo cooks pride themselves on tiny, uniform rolls; the pot is weighed with a plate and served with extra lemon and yogurt. (See food-37-9 for the Palestinian warak dawali.)

Ingredients

  • 1 jar grape leaves + 2 cups short-grain rice
  • 500 g minced lamb + allspice + baharat + salt + pepper
  • Lamb chops/potatoes + garlic + lemon + dried mint + broth

Method

  1. Rinse and blanch leaves; trim stems.
  2. Mix raw rice with lamb, spices and a glug of oil.
  3. Roll small and tight, tucking sides.
  4. Line pot with chops; pack rolls seam-down in snug layers.
  5. Cover with lemony broth; weight with a plate; simmer 50–60 minutes.
  6. Rest; serve with lemon, mint and yogurt.

Chef tips

  • Small and uniform — Aleppo pride is measured in millimeters.
  • Pack tight — loose rolls unravel in the simmer.
  • Weight the pot — a shifting top layer breaks.
  • Lemon at serving too — brightness is the finish.
