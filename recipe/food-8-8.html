Hot Pot

China

China's communal feast: a simmering centerpiece broth (fiery Sichuan ma-la, clear Beijing, or split yuanyang) in which diners swish thin meats, seafood, tofu and greens, then dip in sesame or sesame-oil sauces. The order of cooking is the etiquette.

Ingredients

  • Broth: stock + ginger + scallion + garlic (or store-bought ma-la/tomato base)
  • Thin-sliced beef/lamb; shrimp paste; beef/fish balls
  • Tofu + mushrooms + lotus root + potato; leafy greens; noodles
  • Dips: sesame paste +-or sesame oil with garlic, cilantro, chili, vinegar
  • Portable burner + wide pot + strainers

Method

  1. Set the burner center-table; bring the broth to a lively simmer.
  2. Roots, balls and tofu first — they take minutes and season the broth.
  3. Swish thin meats 10–30 seconds, seafood to just-opaque; never dump everything at once.
  4. Greens go last in small handfuls (30–60 seconds) so they stay vivid.
  5. Finish with noodles in the now-rich broth; dip every bite; top up broth as needed.

Chef tips

  • Thin meat overcooks in under a minute — swish, don't abandon.
  • Keep the broth at a simmer, not a violent boil that clouds and toughens.
  • Separate serving chopsticks keep the communal pot friendly.
